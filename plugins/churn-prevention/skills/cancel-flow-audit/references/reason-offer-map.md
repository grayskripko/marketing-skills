# Reason groups and the one offer each may get

Eight groups, R1 to R8. They line up one to one with the eight-way coding of cancellation reasons many research schemes use (price, value not reached, missing capability, quality, alternative, business event, service, other), so reasons coded elsewhere can be mapped here without re-reading the open text. These skills never code open text themselves.

| Group | Covers | First offer (at most one per session) | When to offer nothing |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 Price or budget | "too expensive", budget cut | A cheaper plan, or a time-limited discount with its end date and full price stated | The customer is closing the business (R6) |
| R2 Value not reached | never set up, rarely used, results not seen | A short setup session or guided restart; pause if use is seasonal | The account never activated and the person asks to leave now |
| R3 Missing capability | needs something the product lacks | Only a workaround that really exists or a feature with a confirmed date; otherwise none | Not built and no confirmed date |
| R4 Quality | bugs, downtime, slowness | A credit for the affected period plus the status of the fix | The fault is unresolved: fix first, do not discount |
| R5 Moved to an alternative | chose another product | None by default; a downgrade only if a lower plan covers what they still use. No discount to match a rival (heuristic) | Usually |
| R6 Business event | closed, merged, downsized, project ended | Pause if the need may return; otherwise none | Business closed or merged |
| R7 Service or support | slow or poor help | A named contact route, and a credit if service levels were missed | An open complaint: resolve first |
| R8 Other or unclear | no reason, "other" | None; the reason screen stays optional | Always |

Rules for every offer:
- One offer per session, shown beside a cancel control that stays visible (CF-05, CF-08). A decline goes straight to confirmation.
- Terms complete: price during the offer, how long, price after (CF-13).
- An R1 discount never goes deeper than the user's stated discount ceiling; win-back offers never go deeper than the cancel-flow offer for the same reason.
- Pause and downgrade carry an end date and an automatic return state the customer sees before accepting.
- No offer to accounts with a chargeback or a fraud flag.
- An account that took an offer more than once in the last 12 months is out of the offer budget; it still gets the normal one-step cancellation.

Sources: own synthesis. Background on why the reason for leaving shapes return: V. Kumar, Y. Bhagwat and X. Zhang (2015), "Regaining 'Lost' Customers", Journal of Marketing 79(4). Recovery after a service failure: D. Tokman, L. Davis and K. Lemon (2007), "The WOW factor", Journal of Retailing 83(1). Cited by name.
