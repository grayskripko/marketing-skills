# Coaching format

Feedback answers three questions in order (layout after Hattie and Timperley, 2007): where the seller was aiming, how the call went against that, and what to do next. It is written to the seller being coached, in plain second person, never about them to someone else.

## Blocks

1. **Aim**: one line naming the call's goal as the seller or user stated it; if none was stated, the default for the call type (discovery: understand the problem well enough to agree a next step; demo: show that named pains can be solved).
2. **Strengths (exactly 2)**: each with the rubric row, the quote and its turn or timestamp, and one sentence on why it helped the buyer talk.
3. **Moments to change (at most 2)**: pick the rows with the largest effect on what the seller learned about the buyer, usually the earliest missed follow-up (often CS-03, CS-04, CS-05 or CS-10). Each moment has four parts:
   - said: the quote, with turn or timestamp;
   - try: one rewritten line the seller could have said at that moment, in their own voice, using only facts from the call;
   - row: the rubric row it moves;
   - why: one sentence on what the buyer's answer would have told the seller.
4. **One drill**: a single behaviour to practise, phrased as a role-play setting that rep-certification can run live ("guarded buyer who states a problem without numbers; I practise sizing it").

## Writing rules

- Coach the behaviour, not the person: "the question at 06:30 moved to who handles it", never "you are bad at discovery".
- A rewrite never adds a fact, number, customer, deadline, scarcity or reference that is not in the user's material (ground rules 3 and 8).
- Rewrites are questions or short statements under about 20 words; one rewrite per moment, no lists of alternatives; the buyer's lines are never rewritten.
- No more than two moments, even when more rows scored 0; list the others under "also seen" by row number only.
- When several calls are reviewed, the moments come from rows missing in k of n calls, with k and n printed.

## Deliberate practice

The drill targets one behaviour at a time with feedback right after, in line with Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Römer (1993) on practice that is focused, repeated and corrected. A drill that bundles several behaviours is split.
