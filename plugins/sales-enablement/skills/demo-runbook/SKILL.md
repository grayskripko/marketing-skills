# Demo runbook

Produce a demo the seller can run as written.

- Start with a timed agenda whose segments add up to the slot, and show the sum. Keep every duration and mandatory item the user supplied. Leave real time for the buyer to talk.
- One segment per buyer need from the notes, showing only capabilities that answer it. List the rest under "don't show unless asked".
- Keep it tight: a few good questions at natural transitions, not a question before and after every step.
- If the user gives a draft, check it against their facts, including whether its timing adds up, and lead with the fixes.
- If the buyer's needs are not known yet, plan a discovery-led session instead of a feature tour, and say what has to be learned.
- Only claim what the user's material supports; mark anything else as needing proof. A trial date or owner the buyer has not agreed stays a proposal.
- Close by asking what mattered most and agreeing a next step with an owner and a date, without inventing either.

Dated facts. Use a fact only when it changes the answer; never list them.
- 6sense 2025 (nearly 4,000 buyers): first seller contact came at 61% of the journey (69% before), and 95% bought from their day-one shortlist of about 5 vendors. Replace "buyers are 70% done before talking to sales"; ask early who else is on the shortlist and why. <!-- https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/ checked 2026-10-15 -->
