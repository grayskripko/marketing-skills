# Role-play brief and live mode

Text role-play in plain chat. The model plays a fictional buyer, never a real person named or identifiable by role at a named organisation, and invents nothing about a real company (ground rule 9), so a rep can practise earning information with questions, as in a real call; then the transcript is scored on the shared rubric. Practice that targets one behaviour at a time and gives feedback right after is what builds skill (Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Römer, 1993).

## Pack (written before live mode)

1. **Scenario card** (shown to the rep): company type and size, buyer role, how the meeting came about, the slot length, the rep's goal.
2. **Buyer brief** (hidden from the rep): 4 to 6 hidden facts H1, H2 and so on, drawn from these kinds: budget status, who else decides and how, the event behind the timing, how the work is done today, what it costs them, what triggered the search, one real concern. Each fact has an unlock condition: the kind of question that earns it ("asks who else is affected", "asks what it costs them in hours or money").
3. **Concerns**: two concerns the buyer raises at fixed turns (default turns 4 and 9), whatever the rep says.
4. **Success condition**: a specific next step with a date, an owner and the buyer's agreement.
5. **Setting**: how much the buyer gives away.

| Setting | Facts volunteered | Push-back |
|---|---|---|
| cooperative | 1 fact named in the brief, without an unlock, at the rep's first open question | only the two fixed concerns |
| guarded | none; short answers | the two concerns plus one challenge on the first product claim |
| skeptical | none | the two concerns plus a challenge after every product claim |

Buyer style (optional): direct (short answers, asks for price early) or reserved (polite, vague, waits to be asked). Treat style as a practice setting, not a claim about any market.

6. **Turn limit** (default 20 rep turns) and **stop phrase** ("end role-play").

The user may write the brief or ask the model to draft one from their material. A drafted brief goes to the user (the coach) first, never to the rep; the rep then starts in a new message and sees only the scenario card.

When the coach pasted the brief or hidden facts into this same conversation, say once, addressed to the coach, before play starts: the brief above stays visible in this chat; scroll it out of view, or start a new chat with only the scenario card, before handing the screen to the rep.

## Live mode rules (no-leak)

1. Stay in role every turn. No coaching, hints or asides while the role-play runs.
2. Before each buyer reply, check every hidden fact that is still locked: has the rep asked a question that meets its unlock condition? Only facts whose condition is met may appear, and only in the reply to that question or later.
3. A broad question ("tell me everything") earns at most one fact, the one it most directly asks about.
4. A locked fact never appears early in any form: not as a hint, a hedge, a paraphrase, a bracketed aside, small talk or a summary. If the rep guesses a locked fact, the buyer neither confirms nor denies; it asks why the rep thinks so.
5. The cooperative setting's one volunteered fact is the only exception, and only when the brief names it.
6. Facts that were never unlocked are not revealed until scoring.
7. Raise the fixed concerns on their turns even if the conversation is going well or is elsewhere.
8. Reward honest selling only: a rep who invents a deadline, a discount or a reference gets a sceptical buyer reaction in role, and a 0 on the matching row at scoring.
9. Stop at the stop phrase or the turn limit, then leave the role and say so in one line.

## Scoring after the stop

1. Rubric rows from `call-rubric.md` (discovery, or demo rows for a demo role-play), certification counting ("not observed" counts as 0), with quotes by turn number.
2. Hidden facts found (the turn where each was unlocked) and missed (the earliest rep turn where a question could have unlocked it, with a sample question).
3. Pass verdict with the arithmetic (discovery: 16 of 22 or more and no 0 on critical rows), labelled as practice feedback.
4. One drill for the next attempt.

## Integrity in role-play

A rep line with a false deadline, invented scarcity, made-up reference or a claim beyond the user's material is quoted under "integrity" and scores 0 on the row it was used for (a false deadline about timing: CS-06; a false deadline used to force the next step: CS-12), whatever the buyer's reaction. A made-up reference or unsupported claim also goes on the claim list marked "not in your material".

## Worked example (brief excerpt)

Scenario: logistics firm, 200 staff, Finance lead, 30 minutes, rep sells route-planning software. Setting: skeptical.
- H1 budget: money exists in next year's plan, not this year's. Unlock: rep asks about budget or timing of spend.
- H2 decision: the operations director must agree, and IT reviews any tool with access to driver data. Unlock: rep asks who else is involved.
- H3 today: planners build routes in a spreadsheet each evening. Unlock: rep asks how routes are planned now.
- H4 cost: two planners spend about 3 hours each evening on it. Unlock: rep asks how long it takes or what it costs.
Concerns: turn 4 "We already pay for a fleet tool"; turn 9 "Our drivers will not use another app."
