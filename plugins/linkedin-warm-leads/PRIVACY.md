# Privacy: LinkedIn Warm Leads

This plugin is a set of instructions. It ships no code and runs no service of its own.

- Categories of personal data: what you explicitly supply or ask it to read for the task. That can be LinkedIn post comments, reactions lists and message threads you paste or screenshot; the one LinkedIn post page you give a URL for; LinkedIn notification emails found by a mail search you ask for (sender, subject, snippet); names you ask it to check in your CRM; and your own LinkedIn data export. These hold other people's names, headlines, employers, job titles, profile links, sometimes email addresses, and the text of comments and messages.
- Purpose: to help you decide whom to talk to and to draft replies and messages that you send yourself, in the current conversation.
- What it does not do: it does not log in to LinkedIn, open any LinkedIn page other than the one URL you give, look anyone up, add data about people from other sources, find or verify email addresses, post or send anything. It does not repeat email addresses or phone numbers in answers unless you ask.
- Recipients: none beyond the AI assistant you are already using, whose provider processes your conversation under its own terms, and the tools you ask it to use (its web fetch for your URL, your mail or CRM connector). The plugin sends nothing anywhere.
- Retention: the plugin stores nothing. A file is written only when you ask for one, in your own working folder; delete it when you no longer need it. Your assistant's own conversation retention applies.
- Controls: paste only what the task needs; do not ask for a mail or CRM search if you do not want one; delete exports, files and the conversation when done.
- Your responsibility: you decide whom to message and remain responsible for lawful use of other people's data (for example under GDPR) and for respecting anyone who asked not to be contacted.
- Network scope: no requests of its own. Your assistant's web fetch opens only a URL you give, and your connectors are searched only when you ask. With a code tool, a short local script may summarise large export files.
