# Advocate program

## A good answer

- Answers the exact question first (for example, what staff should say about working there), then gives the paste-ready wording, template or brief asked for.
- Keeps the user's programme terms (benefits, commissions, eligibility) unless a rule below forces a change, and says which change and why in one sentence.
- Where the facts below do not settle a point (for example whether a badge or public thanks must be disclosed), says what it depends on rather than ruling categorically.
- Audits: which posts need fixing, with the fixed wording for each; no calculation tables, intervals, sanctions or monitoring plans unless asked.
- As short as the request; no extra sections.

## Facts

- US: a connection the audience would not expect (pay, free or discounted product, job, family or business tie, commission) must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously (16 CFR 255.5). Employees disclose in the post; a profile line is not enough. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-255 and https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking (checked 2026-10-03).
- US: "Ad", "advertisement" and "sponsored" are acceptable; avoid "sp", "spon", "collab", a lone "thanks" or "ambassador". In live streams, repeat the disclosure, and a platform's own label may not be enough (FTC, "Disclosures 101"). https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers (checked 2026-10-03).
- UK: "Ad" up front is likely fine; the ASA advises against "sponsored", and "gifted", "#BrandAmbassador" or "#[Brand]Ambassador", a discount code alone or a bio-only note are likely not enough. https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html (checked 2026-10-03). So "Ad" or plain words ("I work at X", "X pays me for referrals") work for both audiences.
- US: rewards conditioned on a review expressing a particular sentiment are banned (16 CFR 465.4). A reward for an honest review of any rating is not banned by that rule but needs a clear disclosure (16 CFR 255.2, Example 9). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-465 (checked 2026-10-03).
- US: inviting only customers who gave very positive feedback to review "may be" unfair or deceptive if the posted reviews end up more positive; inviting all recent buyers is the clean version (16 CFR 255.2, Example 11). Managers who ask staff or relatives for reviews must tell them to disclose (16 CFR 465.5). Same sources, checked 2026-10-03.
- UK: fake reviews and reviews that conceal an incentive are banned from 6 April 2025 (DMCC Act 2024, Schedule 20, paragraph 13); a disclosed incentive is not banned by that paragraph. CMA guidance treats an employee's undisclosed review as a concealed incentive and asking only satisfied customers as possible cherry-picking. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews-cma208 (checked 2026-10-03).
- EU: hiding commercial intent that is not clear from context is a misleading omission (Directive 2005/29/EC, Art. 7(2)); commissioning false reviews or misrepresenting reviews is banned outright (Annex I, point 23c). https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/02005L0029-20220528 (checked 2026-10-03).
- Google Maps: businesses may not offer any incentive for a review or selectively ask for positive ones. Other review sites set their own rules; tell the user to check them. https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114 (checked 2026-10-03).

Newer facts (use a fact only when it changes the answer; never list them):

- US: paying for 5-star reviews breaks 16 CFR 465.4 even if disclosed; reviews a business features in its ads become testimonials and lose the review-hosting exemption. <!-- https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers (Nov 2024), checked 2026-10-15 -->
- UK: a business that republishes reviews on its social media, even ones collected by others, must take reasonable steps to prevent and remove fake or concealed-incentive reviews (a written policy and a checking process). Since 6 April 2025 the CMA can fine up to 10% of worldwide turnover, and hidden social-media advertising is always banned. <!-- https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews/short-guide-for-businesses-publishing-consumer-reviews-and-complying-with-consumer-protection-law (2025-04-04) ; https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/what-businesses-need-to-know-about-unfair-commercial-practices/what-businesses-need-to-know-about-unfair-commercial-practices (2025-12-03) ; checked 2026-10-15 -->
- The FTC's 2024 Rytr order on AI review-writing tools was set aside on 2025-12-22; do not cite it as a ban (fake reviews remain banned under the FTC review rule). <!-- https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/232-3052-rytr-llc-matter checked 2026-10-15 -->

How to use these facts: cite one only when it decides something in the user's case, in plain words with a short source name ("the FTC's endorsement guidance"). Give the URL only if the user asks where a rule comes from. Never print the check dates; they are for maintainers. Do not add a legal-disclaimer block.
