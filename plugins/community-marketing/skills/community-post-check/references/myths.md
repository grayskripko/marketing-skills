# Common claims and what the sources say

Answer from this file in one or two sentences when the user repeats one of these. Never list them as findings on their own.

| Claim | What holds instead | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| "Reddit's 10% rule (or 9:1 rule) is official policy" | The sitewide rules contain no such ratio. A subreddit may set one; read its rules | P-RDT2, P-RDT5 |
| "#ad in my bio covers every post" | Profile and bio notes are likely missed; disclose in each post | US-D101, US-FAQ, UK-ASA |
| "Disclosing at the end of the post is fine" | End-of-post disclosures are likely missed; this plugin puts the disclosure in the opening sentence (plugin policy) | US-D101, POL-FIRST |
| "Employees posting on personal accounts don't need to disclose" | They do, in the post; a profile line naming the employer is not enough | US-255.5-Ex8, US-FAQ |
| "#ambassador is a disclosure" | Alone it is ambiguous in the US, where a brand-named tag is clearer; in the UK even a brand-named ambassador tag is not enough. Use plain words | US-FAQ, UK-ASA, POL-PLAIN |
| "'Sponsored' is a safe label everywhere" | Accepted in the FTC's influencer guide; the UK regulator advises against it. Use "Ad" or plain words | US-D101, UK-ASA |
| "Paying for reviews is fine if we don't ask for positive ones" | US: allowed only with a clear disclosure and no sentiment condition. UK: banned when the incentive is concealed. Many review sites ban any incentive | US-465.4, US-255.2-Ex9, UK-P13, UK-CMA208, P-GMAPS |
| "Paying for any review is illegal" | Not under US Part 465 or the UK Act as such; sentiment conditioning (US) and concealment (UK) are what is banned. Site rules may still ban it | US-465.4, UK-P13 |
| "Asking only happy customers for reviews is just smart" | It may be deceptive in the US and can be cherry-picking in the UK; this plugin never does it (plugin policy) | US-255.2-Ex11, UK-CMA208, POL-GATE |
| "90-9-1 is a law of communities" | A rough pattern from a 2006 article that itself shows other splits (95-5-0.1 for blogs, 99.8-0.2-0.003 for Wikipedia edits). Measure your own | R-NNG |
| "20% DAU/MAU means a healthy community" | No published source supports a universal threshold; define "active" for your community and compare it with its own past | ground rules |
| "More members means a healthier community" | Size says nothing about answered questions, newcomer return or concentration; measure those | community-health |
| "Becoming a moderator is a growth tactic" | Taking moderator actions for an outside reward breaks Reddit's moderator code, and placing your own links that way is content manipulation | P-RDT-MOD5, P-RDT2, RF-8 |
