# Jobs and forces

## Job statement

"When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]." Write it from evidence, with ids. The situation names a trigger, not a demographic.

## Four forces of a switch

| Force | Question it answers | Look for codes |
|---|---|---|
| Push | What made the current way no longer acceptable? | trigger, pain, cost of workaround |
| Pull | What drew them to the new way? | desired outcome |
| Anxiety | What worried them about switching? | anxiety, objection |
| Habit | What kept them with the current way? | habit, workaround |

Use push, pull, anxiety and habit as questions for interpreting supplied switching evidence, not as a proven causal equation. Integrate evidenced forces with ids into the requested job or profile; print a separate four-forces breakdown only when requested or needed to explain the decision. 

Background: the switch timeline and the four forces come from the jobs-to-be-done interviewing practice of Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek; the jobs framing is set out in Christensen, Hall, Dillon and Duncan, "Competing Against Luck" (2016). The wording here is this plugin's own.
