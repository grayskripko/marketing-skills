# How many interviews

There is no universal number. Use these as heuristics, with their conditions.

- For a fixed small round, use the allocated interviews to cover the priority contrasts; review after each call to adjust remaining recruitment. Repetition suggests diminishing returns, not a universal saturation threshold. Record unresolved differences for a further round.
- **Guest, Bunce and Johnson (2006, Field Methods, "How Many Interviews Are Enough?")** analysed 60 interviews with a fairly homogeneous group of women in two West African countries, on one topic; most codes had appeared within the first 12 interviews. The figure depends on those conditions. More varied people or topics need more interviews.
- **"Five users"** comes from usability testing of one design (Jakob Nielsen, "Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users", 18 March 2000, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/, read 2026-10-08). It is about finding usability problems, not about how common a need is. Do not use it to size a market.
- Interviews show what exists and why. They do not show how common it is. For "how many", use a survey or product data after the interviews.
