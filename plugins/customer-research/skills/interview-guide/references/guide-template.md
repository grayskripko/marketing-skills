# Interview guide template

Use these as optional components, not a required output layout. Fit the decision and time budget first. For competing investments, compare recent incidents, frequency, consequences, residual workaround gaps, actual attempts or spending and priority for each. A switching timeline is useful only when switching is relevant. Recruit firsthand workflow knowledge, including negative cases; do not require the proposed pain.

## Research brief

- Default: decision in 1 sentence. Use one sentence per independent decision when the user explicitly supplies multiple decisions; state which decisions the guide covers.
- Default: 3–5 testable hypotheses. Use one per distinct uncertainty in the user’s decision, even outside that range; keep coverage of the decision uncertainties explicit.
- What result would change the decision.

## Who to talk to

Default: screener with 4–6 criteria, prioritising firsthand recent behaviour. Use only the necessary eligibility and contrast criteria when the supplied recruitment pool is small, or add criteria when supplied workflows require extra decision-relevant contrasts. Do not screen on the proposed pain. State recruitment contrasts that affect the decision. Prefer, in this order:
1. people who switched to or from a way of doing the job in the last few months;
2. customers who cancelled recently;
3. active customers who started recently;
4. long-time customers.

## Guide (default 40 minutes within a 30–45 minute slot)

Use the supplied available minutes when different. Select only decision-relevant blocks and set their allocations before writing the guide; the sum must not exceed the slot. Keep a concrete recent incident and a close. Reduce redundant probes first; if necessary narrow the decision and identify what needs a follow-up. Give one timed plan, not a rule explanation for every block. The default allocations below sum to 40 minutes. A 20-minute workflow-priority example with role/team already supplied and no switching or wording decision is 1 warm-up + 13 recent incident + 3 cost + 2 alternatives + 1 close = 20; this is a conditional plan, not a research constant.

1. **Warm-up (default 3 min).** Use 1 min when role/team are supplied; preserve role confirmation and reallocate saved time to the recent incident. 
2. **The last time (default 15 min).** Pick one recent, concrete occasion when they did the job. For a different supplied slot, allocate the remaining time after the other selected blocks; it must be positive. Use switching stages only when switching is relevant. Walk the timeline:
   - first thought that the current way was not good enough, and what happened that day;
   - passive looking: noticing options without acting;
   - active looking: what they searched, asked, compared;
   - the decision, who else was involved, what almost stopped it;
   - first use, and what the first week was like;
   - today.
3. **Forces (default 8 min).** Use 0 additional min when supplied notes or the incident already cover push, pull, anxiety and habit, or switching is irrelevant; spend that time on unresolved decision probes. 
4. **Cost (default 5 min).** Money, hours, errors and stress the job costs now. Reduce to confirmation and missing probes when cost is already documented, or a shorter focused block when the supplied slot requires it.
5. **Alternatives (default 4 min).** What else they tried or considered, and why it did not stick. Ask only missing decision-relevant questions when attempts are documented; shorten this block when the supplied slot requires it.
6. **Their words (default 3 min).** "How would you describe this to a colleague who hasn't seen it?" Use 0 min when wording is irrelevant to the stated decision; retain a focused wording probe when wording is requested.
7. **Close (default 2 min).** Anything we should have asked; who else we should talk to (role, not name). Use 1 min for a shorter supplied slot after reducing redundant probes; retain a closing invitation.
