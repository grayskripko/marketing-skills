# Interview guide template

Use these as optional components, not a required output layout. Fit the decision and time budget first. For competing investments, compare recent incidents, frequency, consequences, residual workaround gaps, actual attempts or spending and priority for each. A switching timeline is useful only when switching is relevant. Draft recruitment criteria for the human researcher that establish firsthand workflow knowledge, including negative cases; do not require the proposed pain.

## Research brief

- Decision this research informs (one sentence).
- Decision-relevant hypotheses, each written so an interview could contradict it; 3 to 5 is an optional short-list example, not a minimum or ceiling.
- What result would change the decision.

## Who to talk to

Draft the minimum criteria that establish firsthand relevance and the required contrasts; 4 to 6 is an optional example. For switching decisions only, consider the following groups without a universal preference order. For other decisions, select recent-workflow and negative-case contrasts that answer the research question:
1. people who switched to or from a way of doing the job in the last few months;
2. customers who cancelled recently;
3. active customers who started recently;
4. long-time customers.

## Example guide budget (30 to 45 minutes; adapt each section to the supplied time and decision)

1. **Warm-up (3 min).** Role, team, what a normal week looks like.
2. **The last time (15 min, example budget).** For switching research, walk a concrete change timeline using the probes below. Otherwise reconstruct a recent task from start to finish, including what went smoothly and conditional difficulty probes; do not assume dissatisfaction or switching.
   - first thought that the current way was not good enough, and what happened that day;
   - passive looking: noticing options without acting;
   - active looking: what they searched, asked, compared;
   - the decision, who else was involved, what almost stopped it;
   - first use, and what the first week was like;
   - today.
3. **Forces (8 min).** Probe the four forces from `../../icp-profile/references/jtbd-forces.md` if the timeline did not cover them: what pushed them away from the old way, what pulled them to the new one, what worried them, and what habit held them back.
4. **Cost (5 min).** Money, hours, errors, stress the job costs them now.
5. **Alternatives (4 min).** What else they tried or considered, and why it did not stick.
6. **Their words (3 min).** "How would you describe this to a colleague who hasn't seen it?"
7. **Close (2 min).** Anything we should have asked; who else we should talk to (role, not name).
