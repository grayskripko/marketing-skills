# Question rules

Each question in a draft guide gets a verdict (keep, rewrite, cut) and the ids of the rules it breaks. A rewrite moves the question toward something the person already did.

| Id | Problem | Example (fictional) | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| QR1 | Future hypothetical | "Would you use a faster invoicing tool?" | "Tell me about the last time you sent invoices. What took longest?" |
| QR2 | Leading: the question carries the answer | "Don't you hate manual entry?" | "How do the numbers get from the bank into your books today?" |
| QR3 | Double-barrelled: two questions in one | "How do you plan and track your budget?" | Split into two questions |
| QR4 | Yes/no where a story is needed | "Is month-end hard?" | "Walk me through your last month-end, day by day." |
| QR5 | Pitch in disguise | "Our tool syncs your bank in seconds — would that help?" | Remove. Describe the product only after the interview, if at all |
| QR6 | Price guess | "How much would you pay for this?" | "What do you pay today, in money or time, to get this done?" |
| QR7 | Jargon the person may not use | "How do you handle reconciliation variance?" | Use their words; ask "what do you call this step?" |
| QR8 | Generalisation instead of an instance | "What do you usually do when…?" | "What did you do the last time…?" |

Output table: #, original question, verdict, rule ids, rewrite.

After the table, give the guide in order with gaps filled: the timeline of the last time they did the job, the current workaround and its cost, alternatives they tried, and a closing question.

Background: questioning about past behaviour rather than opinions or the future follows Rob Fitzpatrick, "The Mom Test" (2013). The wording above is this plugin's own.
