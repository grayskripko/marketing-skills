# Dated rules a voice profile can touch

Stated as of 2026-10-24; each source read on that date. The words in quotes are examples of what to check, never copy to use. This file describes official rules and is not legal advice.

## Words in voice lists that are claims

A voice profile's "words to use" list is repeated across every page the brand writes, so a claim word placed there becomes a claim on every page. Claim words include "eco", "green", "sustainable", "carbon neutral", "guaranteed", "best", "#1", "leading", "proven", "clinically proven", "award-winning" and any number or customer count. In the profile, keep such a word only with a note of the evidence it needs (see `claims.md`), or move it to the never-say list until the user has that evidence.

- European Union: Directive (EU) 2024/825 on empowering consumers for the green transition, applying from 27 September 2026, bans generic environmental claims such as "eco-friendly" or "green" unless the trader can show recognised excellent environmental performance, bans a claim about the whole product when it concerns only a part, and bans "carbon neutral" product claims based on offsetting. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/825/oj
- United Kingdom: the CMA Green Claims Code, published 20 September 2021, sets six principles: claims must be truthful and accurate; clear and unambiguous; must not omit or hide important relevant information; comparisons must be fair and meaningful; claims must consider the full life cycle of the product or service; claims must be substantiated. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-claims-code-making-environmental-claims
- Superlatives, guarantees, numbers and "proven": the evidence each needs is in `claims.md` (dated 2026-10-15).

## AI disclosure, when the profile is for an AI writer

A voice profile written for an AI writer can reach content that falls under disclosure rules. The profile can say when the writer must flag AI involvement; it does not decide whether a disclosure is legally required.

- European Union: AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(4). A deployer that uses AI to generate or manipulate text published to inform the public on matters of public interest discloses that the text is AI-generated, unless a person reviewed it and holds editorial responsibility; image, audio or video that is a deep fake is disclosed as artificially generated or manipulated. Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50 and timeline https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/timeline/timeline-implementation-eu-ai-act
- Meta: ads about social issues, elections or politics must disclose photorealistic images or video, or realistic audio, created or edited with third-party generative AI tools; undisclosed ads are rejected. From 1 June 2026 Meta also detects such media automatically and shows an "AI Info" label in "About this ad". https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/SIEP-advertising/SIEP
- LinkedIn: images and video carrying C2PA Content Credentials show a credentials icon that can reveal AI involvement; in select countries a member uploading media without credentials can self-attest that it was created with AI. https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6282984
- YouTube: creators must disclose realistic content made or altered with AI (a real person shown saying or doing something they did not, altered footage of a real event or place, a realistic scene that never happened) through the "AI use" setting at upload; clearly unrealistic content and minor edits such as filters or captions need no disclosure. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491

If you mention any of these to the user and the read date is more than 6 months old, tell them to re-check the rule on the official page.
