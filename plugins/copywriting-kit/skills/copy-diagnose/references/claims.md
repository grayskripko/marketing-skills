# Claims that need evidence

Shared by all copy skills in this plugin (each skill folder has an identical copy). The words in quotes below appear here as examples of claims to check, never as copy to use.

## Claim types

| Type | Examples | What usually backs it |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers and percentages | "cuts invoice time by 40%", "saves 6 hours a week" | A measurement: who, how many, over what period, compared with what |
| Rankings and superlatives | "#1", "the leading", "the fastest", "best-rated" | A named ranking or test with a date, or nothing (then remove) |
| Comparisons | "twice as fast as spreadsheets", "cheaper than hiring" | A like-for-like comparison the user ran or can cite |
| Guarantees | "guaranteed savings", "never miss a payment" | The actual terms the business offers |
| Customer counts | "trusted by 10,000 teams", "used in 40 countries" | A current count and what counts as a customer |
| Testimonials and endorsements | quotes, star ratings, logos, "as seen in" | A real, identifiable source that agreed to be quoted; any relationship (paid, employee, partner) disclosed |
| Before/after results | "from 10 days to 2 days" | A named case with its scope |
| Urgency and scarcity | "offer ends Friday", "only 3 seats left", "limited time" | A real deadline or limit in the user's plan |
| Awards and certifications | "award-winning", "certified" | The award or certificate name, issuer and year |

## Writing a claim honestly

- **Scope it.** Say where the number comes from: "In one six-week pilot, approval time fell 18%."
- **Match the strength to the evidence.** One case supports "helped one team", not "helps every team".
- **Name the trade-off** where claims are heavy: "Fast to set up for teams on one accounting system; multi-entity setups take longer."
- **Use the real count** instead of a rounded-up one: "212 paying customers", not "hundreds of teams" if the user has the number.
- **Leave a marker** where proof is missing: `[PROOF NEEDED: count of active customers]`.

## When the user wants testimonials but has none

Never write them. Offer a short request the user can send to real customers instead, for example:

> Subject: Two questions about [product]
> Could you tell us, in a sentence or two, what you used before [product] and what changed after you started? May we quote you on our website with your name and role? You can say no, or ask us to leave your name out.

And three interview questions: what problem made you look for a tool; what changed after the first month; what would you tell a colleague who is deciding.

## Background: why evidence matters

Consumer-protection rules in many places expect marketing claims to be backed before they are published and endorsements to be genuine. Examples, named by title only:
- United States: the FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised July 2023); the FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, in effect since 21 October 2024), which bars fake reviews and testimonials; and the FTC's 1984 policy statement on substantiation, which expects a reasonable basis for a claim before it is made.
- European Union: the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (2005/29/EC), as amended by Directive (EU) 2019/2161.
- United Kingdom: the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, whose fake-review provisions have applied since 6 April 2025.

This plugin checks claims against the user's own evidence. It does not give legal advice or decide whether a claim is permitted.
