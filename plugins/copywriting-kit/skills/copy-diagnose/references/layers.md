# The three layers

Most copy that underperforms is broken above the level of words. Rewriting headlines on top of a broken positioning produces the same failure in new wording. Check the layers in this order and stop at the first one that fails.

## L1 Positioning

Questions:
- Who exactly is this for? Can a reader tell within the first screen whether it is meant for them?
- What does it replace? The reader's current alternative: a spreadsheet, a person, another tool, doing nothing.
- Which category does the reader put it in? If the page never says, the reader guesses.
- What is the one differentiator, stated so a competitor could not say the same sentence?

Signs L1 is broken:
- the audience is described as "businesses" or "teams" with no qualifier;
- the headline could sit on a competitor's page unchanged;
- the page lists features before saying what problem they solve;
- two or more audiences are addressed in the first screen.

Fixes belong to the owner, not the copywriter alone: pick the audience, name the alternative, name the differentiator. The report can propose options; it cannot decide the positioning.

## L2 Message priority

Questions:
- How many jobs does the first screen try to do (explain, sell, impress, collect a lead)? One is the target.
- Do the sections follow the order of the reader's questions: what is it, is it for me, how does it work, can I trust it, what does it cost, what do I do next?
- Do the headings alone carry the argument (skim test)?
- Is there one primary call to action, or several competing ones?

Signs L2 is broken:
- three or more calls to action in the first screen with equal weight;
- proof appears only at the bottom, or not at all;
- the "how it works" section is missing or comes after pricing;
- headings are labels ("Features", "Solutions") instead of statements.

## L3 Wording

Questions:
- Is each sentence specific (a number, a named step, a named system) or vague?
- Is the reader the subject, or the company?
- How many stock phrases per 100 words (`stock-phrasing.md`)?
- Does each promise come with a mechanism sentence?

Signs L3 is broken while L1 and L2 hold: the argument is right and in the right order, but sentences are vague, company-centred or padded.

## Verdict wording

- "L1 positioning is the first broken layer: the page does not say who it is for; the headline fits any finance tool."
- "L1 holds; L2 message priority is the first broken layer: the first screen asks for three different actions."
- "L1 and L2 hold; the problems are in L3 wording: 4.2 stock phrases per 100 words and no mechanism for the main promise."
