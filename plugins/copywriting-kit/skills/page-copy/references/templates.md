# Page templates

Section orders designed for readers who skim. Each heading should state a point, not a label. Use the user's facts; where one is missing, put a marker in its place instead of a guess (see SKILL.md, rule 5).

## Landing page

1. **First screen.** Headline: what is sold, or which problem goes away. One sentence under it on who it is for. The one call to action.
2. **The reader's situation.** Two or three sentences in the reader's words: what they do today and what it costs them. From the brief; mark as assumed if not given.
3. **How it works.** Three steps: input → action → result. Concrete, from the facts.
4. **Proof.** Real proof from the brief. If the user said there is none, leave this section out and name what to collect under Gaps; if the user said nothing about proof, use one `[PROOF NEEDED: …]` slot.
5. **Objections and trade-offs.** The two or three questions a careful reader asks, answered honestly, including one limit or fit condition.
6. **Call to action again,** worded the same as in the first screen.

## Product or feature page

1. **What it does,** in one sentence, and for whom.
2. **The problem it removes,** in the reader's words.
3. **How it works:** steps or a short walkthrough, from the facts.
4. **What it connects to or requires** (systems, setup time), from the facts.
5. **Proof,** under the same rule as the landing page.
6. **Limits:** what it does not do.
7. **Call to action.**

## Pricing page

1. **Who each plan is for,** one line per plan, before the numbers.
2. **What is included,** as a comparison of real differences only.
3. **How billing works:** period, what triggers a charge, how to cancel. Only from the user's facts.
4. **Questions buyers ask** about pricing, answered from the facts; unanswered ones go under Gaps.
5. **Call to action** per plan, worded as the next step.

No invented discounts, deadlines or "most popular" labels unless the user states them.

## About page

1. **Why the company exists,** tied to the reader's problem.
2. **Who is behind it:** real people and roles from the brief only.
3. **What the company commits to,** as specific promises ("we reply within one business day") only if the user states them.
4. **How to get in touch** or the next step.

## Headline angles

Write three headline options, each labelled:
- **Outcome:** the result the reader gets.
- **Problem:** the pain that goes away.
- **Mechanism:** how it works, in a few words.

Each must be backed by a brief fact; none may contain an unbacked number or superlative.

## Skim test (only on request)

Print only the headings and calls to action in page order. Then one line: does a reader who reads only these know what it is, whom it is for, why to believe it and what to do next?
