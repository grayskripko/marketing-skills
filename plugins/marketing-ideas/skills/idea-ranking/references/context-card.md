# Context card

Filled before any idea is written. Each field keeps the user's wording; a field the user did not give is either derived (rule shown) or put in the Assumptions box with "change me".

| Field | What goes in | Derived how, when not given |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | what is sold, in one line | from the description |
| Buyer | the role or business type that decides to buy | from the description; never a named person |
| Payer vs user | who pays, who uses, if different | "same" unless the description says otherwise |
| Price and billing | amount and whether monthly, yearly or one-off | assumption, and every grade is capped at D |
| Gross margin | share of the price left after the cost of serving it | assumption, and every grade is capped at D |
| Payback window | months within which a customer should repay what was spent to win them | 12 months, shown as an assumption |
| Buying motion | self-serve, assisted, or sales-led | rule below |
| Demand state | one of four, with the quoted line it rests on | rule below |
| Stage | pre-launch, first customers, repeatable sales, scaling | from customer count and description |
| Cash and hours | marketing cash per month; team hours per week | ask in the questions; meanwhile "not given" |
| Asset inventory | what the team already has that an idea can use | prompts below |
| Tried | what was tried and what happened, in the user's words | "nothing stated" |
| Churn and new customers per period | counts or rates, for the net-growth line | left blank; only used when given |

## Buying motion

- Self-serve: the buyer can sign up and pay alone, without talking to anyone.
- Assisted: the buyer pays alone but usually needs one conversation, a setup call or approval from one other person.
- Sales-led: several people approve, a contract is signed, or procurement is involved.

Decide from the description (a trial button, a "book a demo" step, a contract mention) and write the rule that placed the product, for example "price $39 per month, the owner decides: self-serve". The motion limits the converters an idea may use: a sales-led product needs a conversation somewhere in the path.

## Demand state

| State | Signal in the description | Families it opens first | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Searched-for | buyers already look for this kind of product by name or problem | search capture, paid search, marketplaces and listings, comparison pages | "people search for 'invoice template'" |
| Known pain, not searched | buyers feel the problem but do not look for a product | direct outreach, partners, other people's audiences, events | "owners know dead stock costs money but do not shop for software" |
| New category or low urgency | buyers do not yet see the problem as one to solve | content with distribution, product exposure loops, PR hooks, customer stories | "a new kind of tool nobody budgets for" |
| Few large buyers | a short list of organisations can buy, each worth a lot | direct outreach, events and speaking, partners and resellers | "about 300 hospital groups in the region" |

When the description does not say, pick the most plausible state, quote the line that suggests it, and mark it "assumption, change me". If two states fit, say so and allow families from both.

## Asset inventory prompts

Ask of the description, and list only what is present:
- An audience the team already reaches (a list, a following, a community role, a column).
- Relationships with people who meet the buyers (former employers, suppliers, distributors, associations, consultants).
- Product output that non-users see (documents, reports, links, invites, embeds, printed sheets).
- Data or know-how others would value (a survey, an index, a dataset, years in the trade).
- Existing customers who could refer, appear in a story or co-host.
- Skills inside the team (writing, speaking, video, outbound calling, paid-media handling).
- Access to events, shows, trade press or marketplaces.

An idea that uses an asset scores higher on asset leverage and usually reaches a first signal sooner.

## Tried list

For each item: what was done, for how long, what happened. A clear negative result moves a new idea of the same kind to "Already tried" unless one named change justifies a retry.
