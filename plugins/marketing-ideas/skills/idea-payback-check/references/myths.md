# Myths

Claims users often bring, each with a short correction and its source. No figures from other companies are used.

1. **"A big launch day is a channel."** A launch, a viral post or a press mention arrives once. A channel brings buyers again next month at a cost you can predict. Treat a launch as a wave and plan where it lands (a list, a trial), or it leaves nothing behind. Source: Weinberg and Mares, *Traction* (2015), on testing channels for repeatability.
2. **"Running many channels at once learns faster."** Each channel gets too little volume to read, and the team's hours are split. Test about three cheaply, then put most effort into the one that works. Source: *Traction* (2015).
3. **"Launch platforms and building in public reach any buyer."** They reach the people who visit them, mostly other makers. They suit products whose buyers are makers. No source; check who visits the platform and whether your buyer is there before counting on it.
4. **"A lifetime deal is quick revenue."** It is cash once, then years of support and refunds with no further income from those buyers. Count support hours and refunds against the payback window first.
5. **"A loop that worked will keep working."** Every channel and loop decays as audiences tire and others copy it. Re-read the numbers each quarter. Source: Andrew Chen, essay on declining click-through rates (2012), andrewchen.com.
6. **"Every idea should be A/B tested."** Most small products lack the traffic to compare two versions. A threshold read ("at least k of N in four weeks") is the honest test at low volume. Source: Kohavi, Tang and Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* (2020), on underpowered tests.
7. **"No competitors means an open market."** Usually it means buyers do not pay for this yet. Visible competitors are evidence that people pay, and their pages show how they sell.
8. **"A webinar, a podcast or an article is a channel."** These are converters. Without a source that brings new people every week, they convert nobody. Name the source first.
9. **"Pick the product, then find the channel."** Channels have their own rules and rarely bend to a product; products are shaped to fit a channel. Source: Brian Balfour, "Product Channel Fit Will Make or Break Your Growth Strategy" (2017), brianbalfour.com.
10. **"A free tool markets itself."** It brings the people who search for or share it. If they are not buyers, it adds traffic, not customers. Check who uses it before building more.
11. **"More acquisition fixes slow growth."** When customers leave about as fast as they arrive, net growth stays flat whatever acquisition does. Compute new minus lost before choosing ideas.
12. **"Things that do not scale are a waste of time."** Doing the first sales and onboarding by hand is how a team learns which converter works before paying to scale one. Source: Paul Graham, "Do Things That Don't Scale" (July 2013), paulgraham.com.
