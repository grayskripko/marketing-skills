# Platform and disclosure rules

Each row names the rule, where to read it, the date this plugin last read it, the colour it gives an idea and the compliant variant. When a read date is more than six months before today, the output prints "re-check the current text". This is not legal advice; laws differ by country and change. Jurisdictions covered: US, EU, UK. App-store rows apply only to app products. Rows marked "plugin policy" are this plugin's own limits, not a law. A source marked "unverified" was not read at its official page when this file was written; when it decides something for an idea, tell the user to read the current text. Read dates and row labels stay in this file (ground rule 5).

Colours: **red** = never proposed, declined when asked, compliant variant offered; **amber** = allowed with the conditions printed beside the idea; **green** = no rule found that limits it.

## Reviews and endorsements

| Id | Rule | Source (read 2026-10-08) | Colour | Compliant variant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Reviews or testimonials that misstate who wrote them or whether the writer used the product, including AI-written ones; buying such reviews | FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR 465.2 (in force 21 Oct 2024). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465 | red | ask real customers for honest reviews |
| R2 | Paying or rewarding people for reviews that express a particular sentiment, positive or negative, openly or by implication | 16 CFR 465.4; the FTC's questions and answers on the rule confirm that incentives for honest reviews of any sentiment are not banned. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers | red | amber: an incentive for any honest review, disclosed, after checking the review site's own rules (some sites forbid incentives entirely) |
| R3 | Reviews or testimonials by officers, managers, staff or agents without a clear disclosure of the relationship | 16 CFR 465.5 | red | disclose the relationship, or leave reviews to customers |
| R4 | A review site the business controls presented as independent | 16 CFR 465.6 | red | label it as the business's own |
| R5 | Suppressing reviews by threats or groundless legal claims, or showing only favourable reviews as if they were all of them | 16 CFR 465.7 | red | show all reviews; answer critical ones |
| R6 | Buying or selling followers, views or other signs of social influence that the buyer or seller knows, or should know, are fake and that misstate influence for a commercial purpose; this plugin declines all bought followers, views and votes (plugin policy) | 16 CFR 465.8 | red | grow a real audience; report what is real |
| R7 | Endorsements with a connection the audience would not expect (payment, free product, family, chance to win) must disclose it clearly; this covers creators, affiliates and incentivised reviewers | US: FTC Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising, 16 CFR 255.5 (revised 26 Jul 2023), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255, and the FTC's questions and answers on the guides, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking. UK: influencers' guide to making clear that ads are ads, 3rd edition (March 2023), https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html | amber | disclose in the endorsement itself, in plain words |
| R21 | EU: stating that reviews come from real users without reasonable checks; submitting or commissioning false reviews or endorsements, or misrepresenting reviews | Directive 2005/29/EC, Annex I points 23b and 23c, added by Directive (EU) 2019/2161. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019L2161 | red | ask real customers; say how reviews are checked |
| R22 | UK: fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews | CMA guidance on fake reviews, CMA208 (published 4 Apr 2025), https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews-cma208; the underlying provision of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 is unverified | red | ask real customers; disclose any incentive |
| R23 | Sponsored slots in newsletters, podcasts and sites must be labelled as sponsored | FTC Endorsement Guides questions and answers (link in R7): the disclosure principle applies in all media; newsletters and podcasts are not named there, so this row is the plugin's reading | amber | the publisher labels the slot as sponsored |
| R24 | Referral rewards and affiliate commissions must be disclosed where the recommendation is made | FTC Endorsement Guides questions and answers (link in R7) | amber | disclose the reward; never tie it to a review |

## Messages

| Id | Rule | Source (read 2026-10-08) | Colour | Conditions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R8 | US commercial email: accurate header and subject, identified as an ad (not required when the recipient gave prior affirmative consent), a postal address, a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days; the sender stays responsible for vendors it hires | FTC, CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business; consent exception: 15 U.S.C. 7704(a)(5)(B), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7704 | amber | all of these in every message; the ad label may be left out only for recipients who opted in |
| R9 | UK email marketing: individuals need consent or the existing-customer "soft opt-in"; companies may be emailed without consent; always identify the sender and offer an opt-out. Sole traders count as individuals. Charities also have a soft opt-in for messages about their charitable purposes to people who supported or asked about them, added by section 114 of the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. The regulator notes its email guidance is under review after that Act | ICO guidance on electronic mail marketing under PECR. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/; ICO guidance on the charitable purpose soft opt-in, https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/the-charitable-purpose-soft-opt-in/ | amber | lawful basis per recipient type; sender identity; opt-out |
| R10 | EU: prior consent for marketing email to individuals, an exception for existing customers about similar products with an easy opt-out, no hidden sender and a valid address to opt out; national rules for business recipients differ; personal data also falls under GDPR | Directive 2002/58/EC (ePrivacy), Article 13 (text as published in 2002; later amended, so read the consolidated version). https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32002L0058 | amber | check the recipient country's rules |
| R11 | Messages to purchased lists of individuals with no lawful basis | plugin policy, drawing on the UK and EU consent rules in R9–R10; US law (R8) does not require consent, but this plugin applies the limit everywhere | red | build an opt-in list, or contact businesses where the law allows |

## Platforms

| Id | Rule | Source (read 2026-10-08) | Colour | Compliant variant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R12 | Launch platform: makers may not ask people directly to upvote | Product Hunt launch guide. https://www.producthunt.com/launch | red for vote asks | ask people to visit and comment: amber |
| R13 | Promotions run on a Facebook page need official rules, a release of Meta from liability, a statement that Meta does not sponsor or run the promotion, and may not ask or reward people for sharing, reposting or tagging others | Meta Pages, Groups and Events Policies, section on promotions. https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/pages_groups_events (other platforms: read their own promotion rules) | amber | rules page; entry without sharing or tagging |
| R14 | App metadata may not be packed with trademarked terms or popular app names; another developer's icon, brand or product name may not be used in your app's icon or name without approval | Apple App Store Review Guidelines 2.3.7 and 4.1(c). https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ | red | describe your own app in your own words |
| R15 | Communities' own rules on self-promotion | each community's posted rules | amber | read and follow the rules; disclose that you made the product |
| R25 | Prize promotions (contests, giveaways, prize draws). US: a prize awarded by chance may not require a purchase or payment to enter (otherwise it is a lottery); publish official rules with entry dates, odds, eligibility and the prize value; some states require registration or bonding for large prizes. UK: a prize draw must be free to enter (a free route that is genuinely equal), or be a contest decided by real skill; the advertising code sets rules for terms and winners. EU: rules differ by member state. | UK free draws and skill competitions: Gambling Commission, free draws and prize competitions, https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/public-and-players/guide/page/free-draws-and-prize-competitions (the free route may be no more expensive and no less convenient than the paid one); CAP Code section 8 on promotional marketing, https://www.asa.org.uk/codes-and-rulings/advertising-codes/non-broadcast-code.html. US and EU: unverified, read the current text of the state or national law; the list of what US official rules contain is this plugin's rule of thumb | amber | no purchase necessary or a free route to enter; written rules with dates, odds and eligibility; for a large prize or a new country, check local law first |
