# Wrong practices and myths

These are reported only when they appear in the user's material. If the user asks about one, answer in one or two sentences.

## Wrong practices (ids cited in change logs)
- WP-01 The product is sold in line one instead of naming the reader's problem.
- WP-02 An opener that fits anyone in the segment ("I see you run a finance team").
- WP-03 Invented shared history: a meeting, call, referral or mutual contact the user has not confirmed.
- WP-04 A fake reply or forward subject on a first message.
- WP-05 Several links, images or attachments in the first email; calendar, deck and trial in one message.
- WP-06 A follow-up with no new reason.
- WP-07 A merge field left raw or empty (`{{firstName}}`, `[[company]]`, `%FIRST%`).
- WP-08 The same person emailed under two address variants.
- WP-09 A result with no mechanism.
- WP-10 Invented urgency or scarcity ("offer ends Friday" with no real deadline).
- WP-11 An opt-out that is hidden, removed or made hard to use.

## Myths
- "Business-to-business email is exempt from CAN-SPAM." The FTC's compliance guide says the law makes no exception for business-to-business email.
- "A list of spam words decides where an email lands." Authentication, recipient complaints and sending behaviour matter more (heuristic); a word list is not a delivery plan.
- "Open rates show which email works." Some mail apps load images automatically, which makes opens unreliable; compare replies instead (heuristic).
- "More follow-ups are always better." Each follow-up needs a new reason, and any request to stop ends the sequence.
- "Heavy personalization in every line beats one real detail." One real, sourced detail that relates to the offer reads as written for the person; several generic ones read as automated (heuristic).
