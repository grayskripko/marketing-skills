# Why nobody replies

Work through the stages in this order and stop at the first one the evidence does not clear. A later stage cannot fix an earlier one: a better email does nothing for mail that never arrives, and most people start with the copy because it is the part they can see.

## Is there enough data to call it a problem?

Reply rates are small, so small sends mislead. At the 4.5% average below, zero replies from 30 recipients would still happen about one time in four; from 100 recipients, about one time in a hundred (plain arithmetic, treating recipients as independent). With a small send, say the result is not yet evidence and move on to what can be checked without more sending.

## 1. Delivery: does it arrive?

- Signs: bounces, a sudden fall after sending more, a new domain, no replies of any kind, not even a refusal.
- Cheap check: send the same email to a personal address at another provider and see which folder it lands in.
- If this stage is in doubt, the next step is cold-email-check, which reads pasted records and headers (`../cold-email-check/references/sender-setup.md`).

## 2. List: are these the right people?

- Signs: refusals saying "not my area", replies from the wrong role, one large list with one message.
- Ask who owns the problem at these companies, and why this list now. Fit is judged from what the user says; never find, buy or verify contacts for them.
- A small list written for one segment is the usual test. See the size figure below.

## 3. Offer: is there a reason to answer?

- Signs: polite "not now", or the email could go to any company in the segment.
- Ask what the reader gets, what answering costs them, and whether the first ask is small enough for a stranger. `../cold-email-brief/references/offer-check.md` has the fuller check.

## 4. Copy: does the email say it?

Only now read the wording. Use the rest of this skill to rewrite, and keep every supported number.

## Reference figures

Hunter, "State of Email Outreach 2026 Report", https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email (read 2026-11-06), from 31 million emails sent by Hunter users in 2025:

| Measure | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| Average sequence | 4.5% |
| 21 to 50 recipients, against 500 or more | 6.2%, against 2.4% |
| Open tracking off, against on | 7.4%, against 4.4% |
| Three messages, against one | 6.8%, against 3.3% |
| Two custom attributes in the body, against none | 5.6%, against 3.6% |
| Manually edited, against fully automated | 5.2%, against 4.4% |

These are one vendor's users, and they show what goes together, not what causes what. Quote a figure with its source, and never put one into the user's email or present it as what this user should expect.
