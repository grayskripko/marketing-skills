# Common claims, checked

Short answers for when a user brings one of these up.

1. **"Gate everything; contacts are the point."** Gating trades readers for addresses. When reach, links or citations matter, or the reader is only noticing the problem, an open asset with an optional sign-up usually serves the goal better. magnet-plan's ordered capture rule decides per asset.
2. **"Every extra field costs a fixed percentage of sign-ups."** No reliable fixed figure exists. Published usability research supports asking only for what is needed and labelling optional fields (NN/g, 2016); the size of the effect varies by page and audience, so measure your own.
3. **"An industry average tells us whether our page converts."** Published averages mix traffic sources, offers and counting methods. Compare your magnets with each other on the same source, with intervals.
4. **"A note saying 'unsubscribe anytime' counts as consent."** It does not. In the EU and UK consent is an active choice, such as ticking an empty box (GDPR Art 4(11) and Recital 32; ICO PECR guidance, read 2026-10-03).
5. **"Anyone who downloaded something is a customer for the UK soft opt-in."** The soft opt-in needs a sale or negotiations; the ICO's examples are a trial sign-up, a quote request or a request for details. A download alone is treated here as not enough (ICO guidance on PECR electronic mail, read 2026-10-03).
6. **"Business emails are exempt in the US."** CAN-SPAM covers business-to-business mail as well (FTC CAN-SPAM compliance guide, August 2023, edited January 2024, read 2026-10-03).
7. **"A rounder, bigger subscriber number on the page is harmless."** A count larger than the real one is a misleading claim (US: FTC Act §5, 15 U.S.C. §45(a); EU: Directive 2005/29/EC Art 6; both read 2026-10-03).
8. **"Pre-ticked boxes are fine if the text is clear."** Pre-ticked boxes do not give valid consent in the EU (GDPR Recital 32; CJEU Planet49, 2019-10-01).
9. **"Double opt-in is required by GDPR."** The GDPR text does not require it. It is one way to show that consent was given (GDPR Art 7(1); EDPB 05/2020 paras 104 to 108), and it also confirms the address works. National practice can still expect it: in Germany a confirmed double opt-in is the usual way to prove email consent, from case law rather than statute (BGH I ZR 164/09, judgment of 2011-02-10, read 2026-10-03; register CR-12 where bundled).
10. **"High open rates show interest."** Mail privacy features load messages in the background, so opens are recorded without reading (Apple Mail Privacy Protection, read 2026-10-03). Use replies, clicks and hand-raises.
11. **"The magnet with the higher sign-up rate is the better magnet."** Sign-ups per visit is only the list-growth view. A magnet with fewer sign-ups can bring more sales conversations per visit; read both views (magnet-readout, worked example 1).
12. **"More form fields bring better-qualified leads."** A field with no use before the next step adds a reason to leave and more data to protect (GDPR Art 5(1)(c), register CR-05). Qualify after sign-up, when the reader raises a hand.
13. **"An e-book is the default lead magnet."** The type follows the buyer's stage and the link to the offer; long reads give the slowest first result (magnet-plan S3).
