# Review UK consumer charging and cancellation practices against the supplied terms and produce clause-specific findings and clearer proposed wording

## Compare the promise with the dated treatment

Build an internal timeline from agreement/version, supplied notices, charge due/collected, variation or renewal notice, cancellation request/effective date, service supplied and refund. Separate policy descriptions, proposed charges and actual collection. Match each event to the applicable promise and rule version; do not generalise a single row to all customers.

Assess material mismatches using clause/promise, anonymous case/date evidence, customer effect, deciding rule, proposed practice and matching wording; include these where they answer the request. For adverse variations, compare notice and charge dates with the practical exit time. For an exit offered before an adverse variation, check whether the consumer can actually use it without financial loss or serious inconvenience (CMA37 6.47, p94). Keep ordinary cancellation deductions under their separate test. Renewal remedies are fairness considerations, not universal mandatory notice periods.

Choose the cancellation route before calculating any retention:
- Statutory service cancellation: for an eligible distance/off-premises contract, the ordinary period ends 14 days after agreement day (CCR 30); missing information can extend it (31). Calculate elapsed days and the endpoint from supplied dates. Verify exceptions: only specifically requested urgent-repair visits fall within the narrow repair exclusion; additional services are not swept into it (28).
- Within statutory cancellation, a default-loss or materials-cost row does not establish a deduction. Early service charges need the applicable request/information conditions and proportionate service actually supplied (36). With no supplied service and no early-start request, do not substitute ordinary net-loss compensation. Verify the reimbursement branch and trigger before adding a deadline (34).
- Ordinary consumer default outside statutory rights: assess unavoidable net costs or directly resulting net lost profit after savings, mitigation/resale and duplicate recovery (CMA37 6.62–6.65). Establish what loss fields include before calculating.
- A small genuine binding-reservation deposit has a distinct branch. Check its function, size relative to price, earliest disclosure and precise, clear, narrow retention conditions (6.62, p105). Resale with zero net loss does not automatically defeat this branch. A substantial advance payment is not transformed into such a deposit by its label; no fixed permitted percentage exists.
- Trader breach/cancellation is a separate route. Do not extend a consumer-default or reservation-retention rule to trader failure.

For goods or digital content establish the relevant receipt/supply dates, consent and exceptions instead of transplanting the service trigger. Keep any conclusion conditional only where a missing fact controls it. Terms authorising retention do not establish fairness or statutory entitlement. For refund corrections, show the correct total refund, amount already paid, additional amount due and corrected retained balance; total additional refunds across supplied cases when useful. In statutory-service replacement wording, include both the early-start request and required-information conditions for a proportionate charge. In customer-cancellation wording, expressly exclude cancellation caused by trader breach or failure as well as cancellation initiated by the trader; preserve the consumer’s remedies.

## Worked example

Fictional Rowanfield Repairs retains £75 as an advance payment after ordinary consumer default, outside statutory cancellation and without a binding-reservation deposit. Supplied verified net cancellation loss is £11 after all savings and resale, and no additional loss is claimed. Illustrative refund = £75 − £11 = £64, conditional on £11 being a justified deduction under the applicable cancellation route. Proposed clause: “If you cancel outside any applicable statutory cancellation right, we will explain any reasonable deduction for net loss directly caused by your cancellation, after savings and reasonable steps to reduce it, and refund the balance.” Require the retention process to follow that policy; never present the £11 assumption as a universal fee.
