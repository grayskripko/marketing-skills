# Source-linked intake cards

Use these cards to select records and counsel questions, alongside the supplied facts. Source IDs resolve in [sources.md](sources.md). Read only the relevant card. The matrices are preparation procedures designed here; the source passages substantiate the distinctions, not mandatory intake fields.

## Routing and output

Email format checks apply to Internet messages. Apply federal legal distinctions only when U.S. federal civil proceedings are identified. Use the goods matrix for goods disputes; connect it to UCC law only when U.S. goods law is relevant. The UCC source is model-code text: obtain the state enactment, relevant version and contract terms from counsel or supplied records before stating a state rule. Consultation location does not establish governing law. Use Stripe-specific fields only when Stripe is identified. Otherwise ask which payment rail/provider and accounting system recorded the payment and use the general reconciliation rows.

Use each relevant row to select the exact record to collect and the uncertainty it resolves. Fit those details into the user’s requested table, list or agenda; these are not mandatory output columns. Mark important absent fields “not supplied.” Include only gaps that affect the request; never imply that a hypothetical explanation happened. In a short brief integrate these into records/questions rather than adding a second full report. Cite the source passage when explaining why a specialist distinction matters. Keep the official federal compilation (F0) distinct from the LII reproductions (F1/F2); do not attribute F0’s version date to those pages.

## A. Native records, exports and recoverability

| Trigger / source | Capture or request | How the answer changes |
|---|---|---|
| Email or forward, E1 §§3.6.1, 3.6.4, 3.6.6 | When email provenance or thread reconstruction matters, request the original export with headers (Date with zone, From/Sender, recipients, Message-ID, In-Reply-To, References) and distinguish original and forwarding dates | Date is origination, not proof of receipt. Reply IDs help reconstruct thread relationships; subject alone is insufficient. Resent fields are distinct from ordinary forwarding. Missing headers remain unknown; headers do not establish authenticity. |
| Mentioned attachment, E2 §§5.1, 5.2.1 | Original MIME message, parent message ID, MIME part position/type, available filename, attachment source ID; distinguish embedded message/rfc822 from quoted text | Link schedule/receipt to its parent and distinguish absent upload, omitted export part and embedded original. Preserve the original structure; MIME part order does not supply event order. |
| Electronic document export, F1 Rule 34(b)(2)(E), 2006 note | Source system/custodian, ordinarily maintained format, supplied export format, export date/method, text searchability and any loss of formulas/metadata/attachment links | Return a format/provenance gap beside the missing schedule. Native format is not universally required: ordinarily maintained or reasonably usable forms are alternatives; the note addresses degraded existing searchability.  |
| Reported deletion/loss with U.S. federal context, F2 Rule 37(e), 2015 note | Anticipation-of-litigation facts/date, systems/custodians, retention/deletion settings, loss date, preservation steps, other mailbox copies/backups/recipient copies and restoration attempts | Separate “not uploaded,” “reported lost,” and “restoration/replacement unresolved.” Ask counsel about preservation and recoverability. Rule 37(e) addresses reasonable preservation steps and loss that cannot be restored/replaced; prejudice measures differ from intent-to-deprive findings. Do not infer destruction, intent or sanctions from a missing upload. |

For reported loss, prioritize preserving existing originals/logs and identifying available copies and backups for authorized recovery checks with counsel and IT. Export omission does not prove deletion; a deletion setting does not prove this thread was deleted. Keep loss, recoverability and intent as separate unknowns.

## B. Payment reconciliation

Start with three distinct questions: was money transferred, was it allocated to this invoice, and was it settled to the seller's bank? A receipt or annotation may address only one. These general record requests are workflow choices; provider mechanics below are sourced.

| Trigger / source | Record to collect | Ambiguity resolved / question |
|---|---|---|
| “Balance received” versus deposit-only account | Original receipt attachment and parent email; invoice/account ledger showing posting date, payer, invoice reference, currency, deposit/balance allocation and reversals; complete relevant bank period with coverage dates and account role identified | Which entry caused the annotation, who paid, and to which invoice? A third-party payer or wrong allocation is a possibility to check, not a finding. Partial bank coverage cannot establish absence of payment. |
| Stripe identified, P1 Paid invoices / Out of band invoices | Invoice ID, amount due/paid/remaining, payment references and status history, out-of-band marking and supporting external receipt | A paid invoice or invoice.paid event can occur without a successful PaymentIntent. Ask whether actual payment, credit, or manual external-payment recording caused the status. |
| Stripe credit or adjustment, P2 Credit balances / Transactions; P3 introductory credit-note policy | Customer credit ledger with applied_to_invoice, credit_note and unapplied_from_invoice links; credit-note ID, amount, invoice, reason, creator/approval record and refund/credit allocation | Credit balance is distinct from cash balance. Credits auto-apply to the next finalized invoice rather than a chosen invoice. A credit note can reduce an open invoice to zero and paid status without recording payment. Ask which linked adjustment settled the recorded balance. |
| Stripe settlement mismatch, P4 Balance transaction source / charge-payment types | Balance transaction ID, source ID, reporting_category, gross/fee/net, currency, pending/available status and related reversal/refund records | Pending funds, fees or payment reversals can explain differences without proving nonpayment. Match the underlying payment before comparing net bank receipts. |
| Stripe automatic payout, P5 Find transactions / Download report data / Payout timing | Payout ID and itemized CSV with transaction IDs, payment/refund/dispute/fee rows; bank deposit and report date range including estimated arrival date | Payout groups transactions and adjustments: do not equate one deposit with one invoice. Report uses estimated arrival, not bank posting date. Itemized PDF export is capped at 5,000 rows where available; use CSV to check complete coverage. |
| Stripe manual or instant payout, P5 opening scope | Balance/activity report and transaction history, payout and bank records | Automatic payout matching does not cover manual payouts; Stripe cannot identify constituent transactions for instant payouts. Ask for a separately reconciled transaction history. |

Reconcile only supplied linked rows and currencies. Show gross − fees = net for a transaction if all inputs exist; include supplied signed adjustments when reconciling a payout. Leave unmatched rows unresolved. Never log into a provider, create a credit note or change an invoice.

## C. Goods receipt, use, acceptance and notice

G1 §2-607(1)–(4) distinguishes payment for accepted goods, rejection after acceptance, other remedies, notice after discovery (including when discovery should have occurred), and the buyer's burden for accepted goods. It supplies a reasonable-time standard, not a universal number of days. Acceptance and revocation require counsel's assessment; use alone does not decide either.

| Issue | Capture / exact records | Question it adds |
|---|---|---|
| Shipment versus receipt | Dispatch record, carrier tracking, delivery acknowledgment, receiving log, quantity/batch and recipient | Which units arrived when? Seller's on-time shipment claim does not date buyer receipt. |
| Inspection and discovery | Inspection opportunity/date, tests/photos tied to batch, first symptoms, discovery date and earlier observable signs | When was the defect discovered, and what could have been detected earlier? |
| Use and acceptance-related conduct | Units used/resold/altered, dates before/after discovery, communications approving or objecting, reasons for continued use, assurances of cure | How do these facts affect acceptance, rejection, revocation and other remedies under the applicable enacted text? Keep replacement as the user's goal without declaring an entitlement. Distinguish replacements for reported defective units from fulfillment of missing units; record total installed separately and ask for batch-linked counts, photos and the precise cure promise. |
| Notification | Exact notice content, version, sender/recipient, transmission timestamp/channel, delivery evidence and seller response; preserve undated draft separately | Did the seller receive notice of the asserted breach; what timing/content evidence does counsel need? Date on a notice is not transmission or receipt. |
| Address correction / replacement | Old/new address instructions and acknowledgments, dates, agreed delivery terms, seller replacement/cure proposals and remaining stock | How did the correction affect delivery, and what replacement route fits the facts and contract? |
| Applicable text | Contract governing-law clause, transaction geography and parties, state enactment/version if supplied | Which law governs, and which enacted acceptance/notice/remedy provisions should counsel apply? |
