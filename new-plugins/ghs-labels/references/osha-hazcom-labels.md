# OSHA HazCom label sources

Every URL below was opened on 2026-10-12. The package makes no runtime network requests. These notes give the exact clause and the scope it decides. US federal OSHA Hazard Communication Standard (HCS), 29 CFR 1910.1200, as amended by the final rule of May 20, 2024. State-plan states may add requirements; DOT, EPA (FIFRA), CPSC and FDA labels are separate regimes.

## 1. 29 CFR 1910.1200 — the standard

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200 — read 2026-10-12. Current online regulation.

- (f)(1) Shipped containers leaving the workplace: (i) product identifier, (ii) signal word, (iii) hazard statement(s), (iv) pictogram(s), (v) precautionary statement(s), (vi) name, U.S. address and U.S. telephone number of the chemical manufacturer, importer or other responsible party.
- (f)(2) Items (i)–(v) follow Appendix C for each hazard class and category, prominently displayed, in English; other languages may be added. (f)(3) Signal word, hazard statements and pictograms appear together on the label.
- (f)(4) Solid metal, solid wood, plastic items and whole grain: label may go with the initial shipment only; not for hazardous chemicals used with or present with them.
- (f)(5)(i) No conflict with the Hazardous Materials Transportation Act (DOT). (f)(5)(ii) Bulk shipments: label on the immediate container, or with shipping papers/bills of lading, or electronically with the receiver's agreement so it is immediately available in print at receipt. (f)(5)(iii) Where a DOT-required pictogram appears on a shipped container, the Appendix C.4 pictogram for the same hazard is not required.
- (f)(6) Workplace containers: either the (f)(1)(i)–(v) information, or the product identifier plus words, pictures, symbols or a combination that, with other information immediately available, gives specific hazard information.
- (f)(7) Stationary process containers: signs, placards, process sheets, batch tickets or operating procedures may replace labels if they identify the containers and are readily accessible during each shift.
- (f)(8) No label needed on a portable container filled from a labeled container for the immediate use of the employee who transferred it.
- (f)(9) Do not remove or deface existing labels on incoming containers unless immediately re-marked.
- (f)(10) Workplace labels legible, in English, prominently displayed or readily available each shift.
- (f)(11)(i) Newly aware of significant hazard information: revise the label within six months. Containers released for shipment and awaiting future distribution need not be relabeled, but the updated label must be provided for each individual container with each shipment. (f)(11)(ii) Not currently produced or imported: add the information before the chemical is shipped or introduced into the workplace again.
- (f)(12) Small containers. (i) Only where the manufacturer, importer or distributor can demonstrate that pull-out labels, fold-back labels or tags with full (f)(1) information are not feasible. (ii) ≤100 mL: at minimum product identifier, pictogram(s), signal word, chemical manufacturer's name and phone number, and a statement that full label information is on the immediate outer package. (iii) ≤3 mL, where the party can demonstrate that any label interferes with normal use: no label required, but the container bears at minimum the product identifier. (iv) Immediate outer package: full (f)(1) label information for each chemical inside, and a statement that the small container(s) must be stored in the immediate outer package bearing the complete label when not in use.
- (g)(5) Significant new hazard or protection information: revise the SDS within three months. (g)(6)–(g)(7) Provide the updated SDS with the first shipment after updating, before or at shipment.
- (j) Dates: effective July 19, 2024. Substances: manufacturers, importers, distributors comply by May 19, 2026; employers update alternative workplace labeling, program and training by November 20, 2026. Mixtures: manufacturers, importers, distributors by November 19, 2027; employers by May 19, 2028. Until the applicable date, the 2024 text, the July 1, 2023 text, or both may be followed.
- (b)(3) Laboratories keep incoming labels intact; a laboratory that ships hazardous chemicals labels them as a manufacturer or distributor. (b)(5) Labels under FIFRA, TSCA, FD&C Act, Federal Alcohol Administration Act, CPSA/FHSA and the Federal Seed Act are governed by those laws, not (f).
- (c) "Immediate outer package" = the first package enclosing the container. "Released for shipment" = packaged and labeled in the manner in which it will be distributed or sold. "Product identifier" = the name or number used on the label or SDS.
- (d)(1) Manufacturers and importers classify; (d)(3) mixture classifiers may rely on current ingredient SDSs unless they know or should know they are inaccurate.

## 2. Appendix C — allocation of label elements

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200AppC — read 2026-10-12. Mandatory.

- C.1 The label product identifier is the one used on the SDS.
- C.2.1 Precedence: C.2.1.1 if "Danger" is used, "Warning" does not appear. C.2.1.2 skull and crossbones present → no exclamation mark for acute toxicity. C.2.1.3 corrosion present → no exclamation mark for skin or eye irritation. C.2.1.4 health hazard used for respiratory sensitization → no exclamation mark for skin sensitization or skin/eye irritation. No other pictogram pair has a precedence rule.
- C.2.2 All applicable hazard statements appear; italic text is part of the statement; statements may be combined where appropriate if all hazards are conveyed; one may be omitted only if the responsible party can demonstrate it is inappropriate.
- C.2.3.1 Pictogram = black symbol, white background, red square frame set at a point, frame wide enough to be clearly visible. A red frame without a symbol is not a pictogram and is not permitted. C.2.3.2 Eight symbols: flame, flame over circle, exclamation mark, exploding bomb, corrosion, gas cylinder, health hazard, skull and crossbones. C.2.3.3 HNOC: exclamation mark permitted, not required, with "Hazard Not Otherwise Classified" or "HNOC" below it. C.2.3.4 Each pictogram appears only once.
- C.2.4 Precautionary statements: prevention, response, storage, disposal. Bold = core text; "/" = choose the appropriate phrase(s); "…" = other conditions may be named; italics = conditions for use, not label text; brackets = only in stated circumstances. May be combined or consolidated (C.2.4.6). Minor wording variations allowed if the advice is not diluted, used consistently on label and SDS (C.2.4.7). Similar statements across classes: the most stringent is included (C.2.4.8). Omission only on a demonstration that it is inappropriate (C.2.4.9). Medical response prioritisation and "IF exposed or concerned" for three or more routes (C.2.4.10).
- C.3 Supplementary information must not contradict or cast doubt on the required information or impede it. C.3.3 Ingredient(s) of unknown acute toxicity ≥1% in a mixture not tested as a whole: label and SDS state "X% of the mixture consists of ingredient(s) of unknown acute toxicity", with route where relevant.
- C.4 tables (label elements by class/category). Health: acute toxicity cat 1–2 skull, Danger, "Fatal if …"; cat 3 skull, Danger, "Toxic if …"; cat 4 exclamation, Warning, "Harmful if …". Skin corrosion 1/1A/1B/1C corrosion, Danger, "Causes severe skin burns and eye damage"; skin irritation 2 exclamation, Warning. Serious eye damage 1 corrosion, Danger; eye irritation 2A exclamation, Warning, "Causes serious eye irritation"; 2B no pictogram, Warning, "Causes eye irritation". Respiratory sensitization 1 health hazard, Danger. Skin sensitization 1 exclamation, Warning. Mutagenicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity: 1A/1B health hazard Danger; 2 health hazard Warning. Lactation: no pictogram, no signal word, "May cause harm to breast-fed children". STOT SE 1 health hazard Danger; 2 health hazard Warning; 3 exclamation Warning (respiratory irritation or drowsiness/dizziness). STOT RE 1 health hazard Danger; 2 health hazard Warning. Aspiration 1 health hazard, Danger, "May be fatal if swallowed and enters airways".
- C.4 physical: flammable liquids 1 Danger "Extremely flammable liquid and vapor", 2 Danger "Highly flammable…", 3 Warning "Flammable liquid and vapor" (flame for 1–3); 4 no pictogram, Warning, "Combustible liquid". Flammable gases 1A Danger (pyrophoric gas and chemically unstable gas A/B add statements), 1B Danger flame, 2 no pictogram Warning. Aerosols 1 flame Danger, 2 flame Warning (both with "Pressurized container: may burst if heated"), 3 no pictogram Warning. Oxidizing gas flame over circle Danger. Gases under pressure gas cylinder Warning. Flammable solids 1 Danger, 2 Warning. Self-reactive A bomb Danger; B bomb+flame Danger; C–D flame Danger; E–F flame Warning. Pyrophoric liquids/solids flame Danger. Self-heating 1 Danger, 2 Warning. Water-reactive 1–2 Danger, 3 Warning. Oxidizing liquids/solids 1–2 Danger, 3 Warning (flame over circle). Organic peroxides A bomb Danger; B bomb+flame Danger; C–D flame Danger; E–F flame Warning. Corrosive to metals 1 corrosion, Warning, "May be corrosive to metals". Desensitized explosives 1–2 flame Danger; 3–4 flame Warning. Explosives: unstable and 1.1–1.3 bomb Danger; 1.4 bomb Warning; 1.5 no pictogram Danger; 1.6 none. Combustible dust: no pictogram, Warning. Simple asphyxiant: no pictogram, Warning, "May displace oxygen and cause rapid suffocation".

## 3. Appendix A — mixture cut-offs (used only to flag questions for the classifier)

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200AppA — read 2026-10-12. Mandatory.

- Table A.4.5 sensitizers: respiratory cat 1/1A ≥0.1%, 1B ≥1.0% (solid/liquid) or ≥0.2% (gas); skin cat 1/1A ≥0.1%, 1B ≥1.0%.
- Table A.5.1 mutagens: cat 1 ≥0.1%, cat 2 ≥1.0%.
- Table A.6.1 carcinogens: cat 1 ≥0.1%; cat 2 ≥0.1% → cat 2, where between 0.1% and 1% SDS information is required and a label warning is optional; at ≥1% both.
- Table A.7.1 reproductive toxicants and lactation: ≥0.1%.
- Tables A.8.2 and A.9.3 STOT SE and RE: ≥1.0% for each category.
- A.10.3.3.2 aspiration: sum of cat 1 ingredients ≥10% and kinematic viscosity ≤20.5 mm²/s at 40 °C.
- Tables A.2.3/A.2.4 skin: sum of cat 1 ≥5% → corrosive; acid pH ≤2 or base pH ≥11.5 at ≥1% → cat 1 (non-additivity cases).
- A.1.3 acute toxicity: relevant ingredients ≥1%; unknown acute toxicity ingredient ≥1% triggers the C.3.3 statement.
- A.0.5 bridging principles: dilution, batching, concentration, interpolation, substantially similar mixtures, aerosols. Classification by bridging or test data can legitimately differ from a cut-off sum; the cut-off is a reason to ask, not a verdict.

## 4. Appendix D — SDS sections 1 and 2

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200AppD — read 2026-10-12. Mandatory.

- Section 1: product identifier used on the label; name, U.S. address and phone of the responsible party; emergency phone.
- Section 2: classification; signal word, hazard statements, symbols (black-and-white reproduction or name, e.g. "flame") and precautionary statements; hazards not otherwise classified; unknown acute toxicity percentage statement.
- Section 14 transport is non-mandatory under OSHA. Section 16 gives the date of preparation or last change.

## 5. OSHA Brief "Labels and Pictograms" (DSG BR-3636, 2/2013)

https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3636.pdf — read 2026-10-12. Guidance; does not change the standard. Written for the 2012 text.

- Product identifier may be chemical name, code number or batch number; the same identifier appears on the label and in SDS section 1.
- One signal word only, no matter how many hazards.
- Environmental (aquatic) pictogram is not enforced by OSHA; may appear as supplementary information.
- Workplace labels: Appendix C pictograms may have a black border instead of red; NFPA or HMIS ratings may be used if consistent with the HCS and employees have immediate access to specific hazard information, with training. PPE pictograms are supplementary.
- Inner containers inside a shipped outer package do not need the DOT diamond but do need the OSHA pictograms.
- Its note on Appendix C.2.3.3 and DOT pictograms is superseded by (f)(5)(iii) of the 2024 text.

## 6. Federal Register final rule, May 20, 2024 (89 FR 44144)

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-05-20/html/2024-08568.htm — read 2026-10-12.

- Very small containers are defined by "volume capacity" (3 mL or less), i.e. container capacity, not fill (89 FR 44171).
- Where a DOT pictogram is on the shipped container, the HCS pictogram for the same hazard "may also be provided, but is not required" (89 FR 44151).
- The 2015 enforcement directive stays in force until rescinded or updated; several 2024 changes codify earlier letters of interpretation on small containers and released-for-shipment relabeling (89 FR 44148).

## Plugin rules of thumb (no external source)

- Never derive or change a hazard classification; the approved classification is an input.
- A review with no approved classification, or unreadable artwork, returns what is missing instead of a pass.
- Order of checks: classification match → signal word → pictograms → hazard statements → precautionary statements → identifier and supplier → supplementary information → route-specific items.
