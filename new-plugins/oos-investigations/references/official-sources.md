## FDA — Investigating Out-of-Specification (OOS) Test Results for Pharmaceutical Production, Revision 1

https://www.fda.gov/media/158416/download — guidance dated May 2022; read 2026-10-12.

Scope: chemistry-based laboratory testing of drugs regulated by CDER, including APIs, excipients, components, in-process materials and finished products; contract laboratories included. Biological assays are not its subject (briefly references USP <111>). Excludes in-process tests used only for real-time process adjustment (footnote 2). Footnote 6: much of it may help with out-of-trend results.

- I: OOS covers all results outside specifications or acceptance criteria in applications, DMFs, official compendia or set by the manufacturer, and in-process tests outside established specifications.
- III: investigation required for every OOS (§ 211.192); needed even if the batch is rejected. Phase I assesses laboratory data accuracy, before test preparations are discarded when possible. Contract laboratory sends data and findings to the manufacturer's quality unit, which starts Phase II when no clear laboratory error.
- III.A: system suitability failure means data from the suspect period are not used; retain preparations; obvious errors documented immediately; do not knowingly continue an analysis expected to be invalidated.
- III.B: supervisor assessment, seven steps (method knowledge, raw data, calculations including unauthorized changes to automated calculations, instrument performance, standards and reagents, method performance against validation and history, documentation). Examples of hypothesis testing on retained material: reinjection, examination of an undestroyed dosage unit, further extraction. Laboratory error should be rare. "OOS test results should not be attributed to analytical error without completing an investigation that clearly establishes a laboratory root cause."
- IV: Phase II under a predefined procedure; QU-led; all departments and sites; impact on distributed batches.
- IV.A: written record contents (reason; manufacturing aspects; documentation review with actual or probable cause; prior occurrence; corrective actions). Extension to other batches required. Reprocessing after additional testing needs production and QU comments and signatures. Design flaws call for redesign.
- IV.B.1: retest from the same homogeneous material; second analyst at least as experienced; "testing into compliance" objectionable; maximum retests fixed in advance in an SOP, not adjusted on results; endpoint where batch is evaluated; deviation from SOP needs QU-approved protocol; lab error → retest substitutes but original retained; no lab error → no basis to invalidate; all results reported.
- IV.B.2: resampling for evidence the original sample was not representative or prepared improperly; same validated method; new sampling method approved by QU if original inherently inadequate.
- IV.C.1: averaging appropriate only as defined in the approved method, with variability limits; never original OOS with retests; not for content or blend uniformity; examples 89/90/91 and 80/85/105 against 90–110 %.
- IV.C.2: outlier tests only if predefined in an SOP; not to invalidate a validated chemical test on a homogeneous sample (footnote 15, U.S. v. Barr Laboratories, D.N.J., 4 Feb 1993); no applicability to content uniformity, dissolution, release rate; bioassays per USP <111>. Contract laboratory without assignable cause reports all results to the customer on the certificate of analysis; FDA recommends sharing the OOS report (footnote 16).
- V.A: invalidation only on observation and documentation of a test event that can reasonably be determined to have caused the OOS; confirmed → batch rejection and extension; inconclusive → full consideration, release only after full investigation shows OOS does not reflect the batch; worked example 89.5 % followed by seven retests 98.8–99.1 % against 90.0–110.0 %.
- V.B: series of preparations with mixed OOS and passing values all within method variability → treat average as OOS; replicate injections from one vial meeting variability criteria → single injection does not make result OOS; low passing assay calls for caution (§ 211.101(a), ICH Q1E).
- V.C: field alert report within 3 working days for a distributed NDA/ANDA batch unless the OOS is found invalid within 3 days; follow-up when investigation is complete.

## FDA — Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP, Questions and Answers

https://www.fda.gov/media/119267/download — guidance dated December 2018; read 2026-10-12.

- Q2: invalidating a result requires a valid, documented, scientifically sound justification; the batch record given to the QU includes the original invalidated data and the investigation report.
- Q7–Q8: audit trails are reviewed with the record they belong to; frequency follows the record's review requirement.
- Q13: testing into compliance is not consistent with CGMP; using an actual sample in system suitability, test, prep or equilibration runs to disguise it is violative; all data, including failing and suspect, stay in the record; system suitability per USP <621> uses standard preparations.
- Q14: each reprocessed chromatography result is retained.
- Q15: suspected data falsification handled inside the documented quality system.

## 21 CFR 211.192 — Production record review

## MHRA — Out of Specification & Out of Trend Investigations

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/out-of-specification-investigations — publication page, first published 28 August 2013, last updated 26 February 2018; read 2026-10-12.
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a82373b40f0b62305b93186/MHRA_OOS_OOT_Oct17.pptx — guidance file dated October 2017; read 2026-10-12.

UK GMP expectations presented as a flowchart; complementary to the FDA guidance. Definitions of OOS, OOT, atypical result, assignable cause, reportable result, hypothesis testing, retest, resample. Phase Ia: obvious errors (calculation error, power outage, equipment failure, spilled sample, wrong instrument parameter) documented and corrected. Phase Ib: analyst and supervisor checklist (method version, sample identity and custody, calibration, standards, system suitability before and during, glassware, specification applied, reagents in date, training, raw data, previous issues, other batches in the run, method validation) plus microbiology considerations. Hypothesis testing needs a written plan approved by QA or the contract giver first and may not replace the original result. Phase II: manufacturing investigation before further testing; retest plan predefined, second analyst; analyst error triggers review of that analyst's other analyses using the same technique; averaging cautions; 95 % confidence limit table (t by sample size n: 2 → 12.71 … 7 → 2.45, 10 → 2.26, 20 → 2.09); resampling "should rarely occur"; outlier tests cannot justify rejecting data for validated chemical tests on homogeneous samples. Staged compendial tests: the next stage is not a retest but needing it unusually should be investigated. Phase III: impact on other batches, stability, validated processes and methods; rejection cannot be reversed by further testing. Inconclusive: full consideration by the certifying QP.

## MHRA Inspectorate blog — Out of Specification Guidance

https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2018/03/02/out-of-specification-guidance/ — posted 2 March 2018; read 2026-10-12.

States the most common deficiency inspectors raise is an OOS result invalidated "on the basis of assumptions and theories with no real evidence"; criticises the habit of repeating the test twice and hoping for two passes; promotes hypothesis testing.
