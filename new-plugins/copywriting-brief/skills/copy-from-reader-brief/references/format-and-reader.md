## Google responsive search ads

Source: [Google Ads Help: About responsive search ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791?hl=en), checked 2026-10-11; publication/update date not displayed. Applies to responsive search ads, not display, social or every Google ad format.

Source facts: enter 3–15 headlines and 2–4 descriptions. Headline limit: 30 characters; description: 90; each display-path field: 15. Double-width characters count as 2. Assets can appear in different orders; text can be shortened. Required text must be pinned to Headline position 1, Headline position 2 or Description position 1. Headline position 3 and Description position 2 are not guaranteed. Multiple assets pinned to the same position alternate. Unused assets can also serve as links, including assets borrowed within an ad group under enhanced flexibility. Pinning restricts combinations and can affect Ad Strength; these facts do not promise performance.

Drafting/review decisions (editorial application): count final asset text including spaces and punctuation; use the stated double-width convention where relevant. Show each count beside the asset, outside upload text. Keep each claim self-contained with its essential limitation. Avoid dependent fragments and pronouns requiring another asset. If a restriction must always show, give a supported pinning note; every alternate in that slot must carry it. Do not rely on a separate optional asset to rescue a misleading claim. An accurate starting price may be qualified by adjacent text guaranteed in every served combination, such as a sole Description position 1 asset stating that transport is charged separately; do not require that condition in every price asset as well. This does not permit a false claim or a qualification that can disappear. In reviews, check the remaining combinations for product identification as well as restrictions, and limit findings to the supplied assets and setup. Preview review and ad-group settings remain outside a text-only job.

## Product-category reader information

Source: [Baymard product-description research](https://baymard.com/research-articles/product-descriptions), checked 2026-10-11; publication/update date not established from the loaded page. Original usability observations, not platform policy or universal conversion estimates.

Source observations: personal-care shoppers sought ingredients; unlabeled chair dimensions made comparison difficult. Composition and clearly identified dimensions can matter to product selection.

Decisions and worked examples (editorial application): name missing composition in the requested ingredients line; use plain missing-information wording in finished copy or “Ingredients: [DETAIL NEEDED: complete supplied ingredient list]” in a fill-in draft; do not infer allergy safety or “free from” claims. For a chair described as “compact,” ask for labeled overall dimensions and relevant seat dimensions, with units, rather than inventing fit. Suggested output: “Overall dimensions: [DETAIL NEEDED: labeled width, depth and height with units].” Explain the specific choice blocked by the missing information. These are category prompts to use when relevant to the named reader’s task; they do not prescribe an exhaustive questionnaire or forecast sales. For an additions-only size review, put confirmed labels, units and relevant clearance measurements beside the size entry and before the choice action. Flag unverified measurements and unsupported universal-fit wording at that location; retain unrelated studio copy. Do not add delivery or returns questions unless they affect the requested decision. For a short product-page draft, keep missing ingredients and delivery information in their requested lines without a second editorial note.
