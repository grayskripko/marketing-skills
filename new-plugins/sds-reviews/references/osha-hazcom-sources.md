# Official sources for SDS reviews

All sources were opened on 2026-10-12. Scope: US federal OSHA Hazard Communication Standard (HazCom), 29 CFR 1910.1200, as amended 2024-05-20 and with compliance dates extended 2026-01-15. State Plan states may add requirements. The package makes no network requests; these links are for the reader.

## 1. Hazard Communication Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1200

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200 (read 2026-10-12; amendment history lists 89 FR 44144, 2024-05-20, and 91 FR 1696, 2026-01-15)

- (c) Product identifier: the name or number used on a label or in the SDS; it must allow cross-reference among the hazardous chemical list, label and SDS. (e)(1)(i): the list uses a product identifier referenced on the SDS.
- (b)(6)(ix): consumer product exemption only when used at work for the intended consumer purpose with consumer-level duration and frequency of exposure.
- (d)(1)(i): classification includes a change in physical form and reaction products of known or reasonably anticipated uses. (d)(1)(ii): employers need not classify if they rely on the manufacturer's or importer's classification.
- (f)(11): labels revised within six months of new significant hazard information.
- (g)(1): SDS for each hazardous chemical; employers keep one for each hazardous chemical used.
- (g)(2): English; 16 numbered headings in order. Note 1: headings 12-15 must appear for GHS consistency. Note 2: OSHA does not enforce content of sections 12-15.
- (g)(3): where no relevant information is found for a subheading, the SDS must say so.
- (g)(5): significant new hazard or protection information added within three months.
- (g)(6), (g)(7): SDS with the initial shipment and the first shipment after an update; on request.
- (g)(8), (g)(10): readily accessible during each shift; any form, including electronic.
- (h)(1): training at initial assignment and whenever a new chemical hazard is introduced into the work area.
- (i)(1)(iv)-(vi): prescribed trade-secret concentration ranges A-M (0.1-1, 0.5-1.5, 1-5, 3-7, 5-10, 7-13, 10-30, 15-40, 30-60, 45-70, 60-80, 65-85, 80-100 %); narrowest possible; two consecutive ranges A-G may be combined between 0.1 % and 30 %; narrower ranges allowed.
- (j): effective 2024-07-19. Substances: suppliers 2026-05-19, employers 2026-11-20. Mixtures: suppliers 2027-11-19, employers 2028-05-19. Until then the 2012 text, the 2024 text or both may be followed.

## 2. Appendix D, Table D.1 (minimum SDS content)

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200AppD (read 2026-10-12)

Sections 1-11 and 16 mandatory; order within a section free; "no applicable information" must be stated. Section 1 requires U.S. address and U.S. phone. Section 2(e): unknown acute toxicity statement for ingredients at 1 % or more when the mixture was not tested as a whole. Section 3: exact percentages except trade secret, batch variability or similar mixtures; source of identifier when CAS is missing. Section 8: PEL, TLV and other limits for every Section 3 ingredient, where available. Section 9: 18 properties (a)-(r) incl. kinematic viscosity and particle characteristics. Section 11(f): NTP, IARC, OSHA carcinogen status. Section 16: date of preparation or last change. Footnote: boiling point per 1910.106(a)(5); note in sections 7 and 9 if an alternate storage calculation differs from Section 2.

## 3. Appendix A, health hazard criteria

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200AppA (read 2026-10-12)

Table A.1.1 acute toxicity cut-offs (oral, dermal, gases, vapors, dusts/mists). A.2.2.5 / A.2.3.1.3 and A.3.2.2.4 / A.3.3.1.2: pH 2 or below or 11.5 or above is considered Skin Corrosion Category 1 and Serious Eye Damage Category 1 in the absence of other information; acid/alkaline reserve data may show otherwise. ## 4. Appendix B, physical hazard criteria

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200AppB (read 2026-10-12)

Table B.6.1 flammable liquids: Cat 1 FP < 23 °C and IBP ≤ 35 °C; Cat 2 FP < 23 °C and IBP > 35 °C; Cat 3 FP ≥ 23 °C and ≤ 60 °C; Cat 4 FP > 60 °C and ≤ 93 °C. B.3 aerosols Categories 1-3; B.17 desensitized explosives.

## 5. Appendix C, label elements

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200AppC (read 2026-10-12)

C.2.1.1 "Danger" excludes "Warning". C.2.1.2-C.2.1.4 pictogram precedence (skull and crossbones, corrosion, health hazard over exclamation mark for the named classes). C.2.3.4 each pictogram once. C.2.4.7 precautionary wording consistent on label and SDS.

## 6. 2024 final rule, 89 FR 44144

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-05-20/html/2024-08568.htm (read 2026-10-12)

Preamble on Appendix D: U.S. address and phone in Section 1 clarifies an existing requirement; foreign suppliers shipping to US importers need not give their own U.S. contact; the importer is the responsible party. Section 9: "appearance" replaced by physical state and color; odor threshold retained under odor; evaporation rate retained under vapor pressure; "kinematic" viscosity; particle characteristics for solids; property order on the SDS is free. Released-for-shipment date on labels was proposed and not adopted. Unknown acute toxicity statement applies on label and SDS.

## 8. OSHA interpretation letters

- 2014-06-13, https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2014-06-13 (read 2026-10-12): employers keep the most recent SDS received; not cited for holding an MSDS when no SDS was received; suppliers must provide on request.
- 2015-07-27, https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2015-07-27 (read 2026-10-12): MSDS for product received before 2015-06-01 acceptable if HCS 1994 compliant and no SDS since received; if no SDS after a good-faith request, the employer may contact the OSHA Area Office.
- 2015-08-13, https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2015-08-13 (read 2026-10-12): decimal comma acceptable with an explanatory note.
- 1987-10-01, https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/1987-10-01 (read 2026-10-12): superseded sheet for a mixture may be discarded when the new one lists the same hazardous chemicals; otherwise 30-year retention as an exposure record, or keep a record of identity, where and when used.

Interpretation letters explain requirements; they do not create new ones. Older letters may predate the 2024 amendments.

## 9. Access to employee exposure and medical records, 29 CFR 1910.1020

https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1020 (read 2026-10-12)

## 11. OSHA Brief, Hazard Communication Standard: Safety Data Sheets (OSHA 3514)

https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3514.pdf (read 2026-10-12)

Plain-language description of the 16 sections; "no applicable information" must be stated; sections 12-15 not enforced. Written for the 2012 text: it still shows "appearance" and does not show the 2024 Section 9 list or the U.S. address wording. Use sources 1-2 for current content.

## Rules of thumb of this package (no external source)

- Finding order (contradiction, omission, format, question), the "2024 update item" label, the "not reviewable from supplied text" marker and the supplier-question format.
- Treating "no data available" as a question only when other SDS data make it implausible.
- Matching inventory lines to SDSs on name plus code plus supplier, and reporting look-alike names as identity mismatches.
- Not computing mixture ATEs or new classifications; reporting data-classification mismatches as supplier questions.
- EU-format SDS observations (REACH numbers, UFI, EUH statements not required under OSHA) are a reviewing convention; EU REACH Annex II itself is not applied.
