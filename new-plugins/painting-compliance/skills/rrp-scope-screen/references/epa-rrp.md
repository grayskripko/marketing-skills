## EPA overview and rule history

- [Contractors](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program-contractors), read 2026-10-11; page updated March 31, 2026. Sections “Renovation Program Overview,” “Activities Covered” and “Paint testing” support pre-1978 paid-work coverage, rental managers, minor-area limits, component testing by qualified people and the option to assume lead rather than test.
- [Program rules](https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-renovation-repair-and-painting-program-rules), read 2026-10-11; page updated December 15, 2025. History/index includes 2008 rule, 2010 opt-out removal and subsequent amendments. Use current regulatory sections below for decisions; old owner opt-out forms do not establish an exemption. Public/commercial rulemaking material does not make every commercial renovation covered.

## Area, building and work gates

- [40 CFR 745.83](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-745/subpart-E/section-745.83), read 2026-10-11. “Minor repair and maintenance activities”: ≤6 sq ft per room interior; ≤20 exterior; exclusions for windows, painted demolition and prohibited/restricted practices. Removal counts full removed painted surface; nonemergency same-room jobs in 30 days aggregate. “Child-occupied facility”: same child under six, two weekly days, ≥3 hours daily, ≥6 weekly, ≥60 annual; includes routine common areas/adjacent exteriors, excludes passage-only common areas.
- [40 CFR 745.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-745/subpart-F/section-745.103), read 2026-10-11. “Target housing” and “0-bedroom dwelling”: pre-1978 housing with elderly/disability/zero-bedroom exception unless an under-six child resides or is expected. Current definition reflects November 12, 2024 amendment, important for studio screening. “Lead-based paint”: 1.0 mg/cm² or 0.5% by weight.
- [40 CFR 745.85](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-745/subpart-E/section-745.85), read 2026-10-11. Paragraph (a)(3): no open flame; high-speed equipment needs shroud/containment and HEPA attachment capturing at generation with no visible escape; heat-gun temperature below 1,100°F. Paragraph (d) distinguishes paint application after completed preparation/verification from paint-disturbing work.

## Evidence and emergency gates

- [40 CFR 745.82](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-745/subpart-E/section-745.82), read 2026-10-11. (a)(1) written certified inspector/risk-assessor determination obtained by firm; (a)(2) certified renovator's recognized kit per instructions on affected components; (a)(3) certified-renovator chips analyzed by recognized lab. Kit/chip integrated-whole sampling exception does not cover separately refinished parts. (b) defines unplanned emergency and extent-based relief; cleaning, verification and specified records remain; elevated-blood-lead interim controls differ from other emergency response.
- [EPA Lead Test Kits](https://www.epa.gov/lead/testkits), read 2026-10-11; page updated November 6, 2025. Requested `/lead/lead-test-kits` redirects here. EPA recognizes Luxfer Magtech LeadCheck and D-Lead for wood, ferrous metal, drywall/plaster; Massachusetts kit for drywall/plaster only. September 2024 update preserves recognition after LeadCheck transfer from 3M to Luxfer Magtech. Existing 3M stock may be used with shelf-life checks. Negative results concern regulated paint, not zero trace lead. EPA says no kit has yet met both recognition performance criteria; do not infer that recognition requires perfect positives.

## Authority and paperwork consequence

- [EPA Firm Certification](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program-firm-certification), read 2026-10-11. “Determine if You Apply” lists 15 authorized states and Minnesota Chippewa Tribe–Bois Forte, with official authority links. Do not infer another Tribal jurisdiction from state alone. “RRP Rule Requires Firm Certification” gives five-year EPA firm certification; individual training and firm certification are distinct.
- [40 CFR 745.84](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-745/subpart-E/section-745.84), read 2026-10-11. Owner/occupant/facility-specific Renovate Right delivery routes and 60-day/seven-day timing; common-area/facility notification alternatives.
- [40 CFR 745.86](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-745/subpart-E/section-745.86), read 2026-10-11. Retain applicable records for three years after completion, including no-lead determinations. Covered-work records include receipt/delivery, assigned renovator/training, practice and verification evidence; customer information by earlier final invoice or completion +30 days.

## Interpretation and output choices

The five decision labels, component table, missing-detail marker and one-question answer shape are editorial workflow choices. They are not EPA status codes. A negative test on only one component does not establish a whole-project exception; apply evidence to its actual scope. An urgent planned job is not necessarily an emergency. A historical rule-history page supports provenance, not the current wording of a deciding rule.

## Negative-test reporting clarification

[EPA negative-test exception FAQ](https://www.epa.gov/lead/if-certified-renovator-using-epa-recognized-lead-test-kit-or-paint-chip-sampling-determines), read 2026-10-11; updated September 30, 2026. The exempt renovation may be performed without the usual certified-firm/work-practice packet, but the certified renovator’s testing firm remains subject to .86(a), (b)(1)(ii)/(iii) and (c): retain test records three years and report applicable test information. EPA recommends a separate exempt renovation firm retain a copy. Match obligation to the firm’s role; no-lead means less paperwork, not no testing-firm reporting. Emergency relief in .82 does not exempt .86(c) reporting; apply the normal earlier invoice/completion +30-day deadline to surviving compliance information.

## Rebuild verification — read 2026-10-11

- [Emergency applicability, §745.82(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/section-745.82): non-interim emergency relief is limited to warning signs, containment, waste handling, training and certification as necessary for response. Elevated-blood-lead interim controls receive only information-distribution relief. Cleaning requires a certified renovator or a worker trained by one; verification requires a certified renovator. Removal-method restrictions are not among the relieved categories.
- [Child-facility notice, §745.84(c)(2)–(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/section-745.84): both parent/guardian routes require nature, locations, anticipated completion date and free compliance-record access; signs also need the pamphlet or free-access instructions. Keep the signed dated notification statement.
- [Records, §745.86(a), (b)(6), (c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-R/part-745/subpart-E/section-745.86): record emergency nature and omitted provisions; retain applicable records three years after completion; customer information is due at earlier final-invoice delivery or completion +30 days.
- [Target-housing definition, §745.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/section-745.103): the November 12, 2024 amendment note supports the current zero-bedroom child exception. The EPA Program Rules history lists earlier RRP amendments; it does not supply this 2024 definition amendment.
