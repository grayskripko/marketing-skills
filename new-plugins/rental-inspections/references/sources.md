## Sources, status and deciding passages

### 1. MHCLG electrical safety guidance

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance

Published 1 November 2025; publication landing page also opened:
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance

Read 2026-10-11. Current published operational guidance at review. Sections 2–4 distinguish tenancy scope, fixed installations, landlord-provided social equipment, record fields and delivery. C1 danger needs action; C2 requires remediation; FI needs investigation; C3 recommends improvement. Section 8 covers the first-check transition, valid reports on re-letting, EICs for new builds/full rewires, access constraints and periodic tenancy types. The guidance's broad summaries are reconciled with the specific statutory duties below; do not use guidance simplification to erase a written-request trigger or invent an equipment delivery duty.

### 2. Original 2020 regulations, as made

https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/312/pdfs/uksi_20200312_en.pdf

Made 18 March 2020; commenced 1 June 2020. Read 2026-10-11. Historical base text, read **with the 2025 amendments**, not as today's unamended regime. Regulation 3(3) gives tenant/report duties; 3(4) sets a work period starting with inspection date, and 3(5) requires post-work confirmation plus original report to tenant and council. Paragraph 3(6) repeats steps for further necessary work. Council and prospective-tenant report requests are written requests. Schedule 1 has a seven-year exclusion qualified by an earlier termination option and a precise resident-landlord shared-amenity exclusion. Original social-provider exclusion is removed by 2025 amendments.

Deadline distinction: work period starting with inspection date is inclusive (date + N − 1); copies within N days of an event use event date + N. These are calendar-day clocks. Follow a necessary shorter report period or explicit urgent action; none authorises leaving a C1 dangerous.

### 3. Social-sector extension, 2025 No. 1043, as made

https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/1043/pdfs/uksi_20251043_en.pdf

Made 23 September 2025. Read 2026-10-11. Regulation 1 splits commencement: 1 November 2025 for other purposes; 1 May 2026 for relevant duties for registered-provider tenancies granted before 1 December 2025. The exact boundary includes **1 December itself** in the newer group; guidance's “after” wording is incomplete. Regulation 7 inserts 3E requiring both first checks before 1 November 2026 for the older group, and removes their later-commencement equipment-work alternative.

Regulation 5 changes installation reports, earlier dates, reasonable steps and retention until next inspection is due or actually conducted, whichever is later, unless superseded. Social reports include item-by-item equipment-check currency for items identified to the inspector.

Regulation 6 inserts equipment duties 3A–3D. Their scope is a registered provider's supplied equipment under a social-housing tenancy. The qualified person selects visual inspection/testing; safe items need a next date, at most five years or earlier. Unsafe periodic-check items require immediate tenant notification and prompt remediation/replacement no later than 28 days **after** the check. Under 3C(3)(b), unoccupied ordinary pre-tenancy cases allow before commencement **if later**; guidance simplifies this as before commencement. Unsafe use at occupation is not an acceptable plan. After remedy/replacement, 3C(4) requires a new safe-use record and next date.

Equipment 3D distinguishes request-driven existing/prospective delivery (28 days, written request), before-occupation new-tenant delivery, and council equipment identification plus records (seven days, written request). It also supplies records to next check/remedial person and identification plus records to the installation inspector. These specific equipment provisions do not copy installation 3(5)'s automatic post-work council confirmation duty. Guidance section 5's broad combined paragraph should not be presented as an additional statutory equipment clock. Honour a supplied council direction or voluntary guidance-following plan with its actual attribution.

### 4. Proposed 2026 amendment: draft, not treated as operative

https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2026/9780348285703/pdfs/ukdsi_9780348285703_en.pdf

Read 2026-10-11. Official draft document, with blank made date and proposed commencement 30 November 2026. Regulations 3–5 update the standard reference, clarify current report/record reuse and remove investigation wording. Regulation 9 preserves the previous investigation provisions for inspections completed before commencement. This is a maintenance trigger, not a basis to stop acting on October FI findings. For affected later cases verify the final made instrument, its dates and saving provision before changing the October rules. Search during this build did not establish a final made number.

### 5. Regulator of Social Housing: Safety and Quality Standard

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/safety-and-quality-standard

Published 2 April 2024; effective 1 April 2024. Read 2026-10-11. Paragraphs 2.1.1, 2.2.1–2.2.2 require property-level condition evidence, meeting applicable health/safety requirements and completing assessment actions on appropriate timescales. Paragraph 2.3.5 includes communal maintenance. This supports keeping communal areas outside this specific inspection regime without implying that communal safety can be ignored. Do not invent an additional EICR/PAT interval from this standard.

### 6. MHCLG Awaab's Law Phase 2 guidance

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-phase-2-guidance-for-social-housing-landlords/awaabs-law-phase-2-guidance-for-social-landlords

Updated 31 July 2026. Read 2026-10-11. The introduction separates the emergency-hazard duties already in force from 27 October 2025 from the planned wider significant-hazard categories from 30 November 2026. Section 1.5 excludes occupation under a licence from Awaab's Law, unlike the licence-inclusive electrical regime. Apply that separate tenancy scope before assigning its statutory deadlines or accommodation duties. Sections 4.3 and 6 cover suspected emergency hazards, including exposed wiring: investigate and carry out necessary safety work as soon as practicable within 24 hours of awareness. Section 9 requires suitable alternative accommodation at landlord expense if necessary safety work cannot be completed within the initial period. Code labels alone do not establish the hazard threshold. Routine rented-sector 28-day work limits do not displace emergency action. This register does not apply the future significant-electrical-hazard timetable to October cases.

## Review choices

Using an action table, aliases and missing-detail markers is this plugin's rule of thumb, not a statutory report format. Recommended item fields in guidance are not all mandatory legal fields. Sources support dated evidence review, not technical defect recoding or physical safety certification.

Keep the October submission check separate from later changes. Refresh the 2026 draft status and MHCLG guidance before release around 18 October and before applying changed provisions from 30 November. Actual source verification in this build occurred on 11 October.
