# Late Payment Charges

## Check and calculate charges

Answer the user’s charge question or outstanding-total question directly, then show the relevant amounts and calculation. When a charge notice is requested alongside a calculation, give the amount remaining first and a separate ready-to-use notice with the supplied payment reference, or the original invoice reference if no separate reference was supplied. Mark unresolved charge items conditional instead of putting guessed amounts into a demand. For paid-late invoices show the remaining charges separately from zero unpaid principal.

Extract debtor type, governing-law scope, VAT-inclusive original debt, contract/due-date basis, interest clause, receipt/delivery dates when relevant, as-of/payment date, dated part-payments/credits, disputes and prior compensation. Do useful parts first and ask one deciding question by default for a single missing essential; batch independent deciding inputs by invoice or shared contract when several are absent. Separate each qualifying debt in an export, deduplicate reissued copies and reconcile totals; do not assume every row permits a fixed charge.

Apply the inline rules below. Calculate with a reliable date/decimal tool available in the host, or carefully verified arithmetic; no bundled calculator is provided. Check the formula, rate reference date, period boundaries and final rounding. For default timing, make the inclusive count visible: trigger date is day 1, trigger + 29 is day 30/due date, and trigger + 30 is the first interest day. When asked how a part-payment changes the calculation, explain the drop in daily interest alongside the balance segments; quantify the saving over the remaining period if useful, retaining full precision. For continuing interest, tell the user to recompute the full accrued amount at the payment date and round once, rather than repeatedly add a rounded daily amount. If supplying charge-invoice wording, itemise original principal as the calculation basis and show only new charges as new amounts payable.

## Decide the branch before calculating

For a full calculation, use this default dependency order: commercial eligibility and governing law; contractual remedy; valid due date; first late day and its historical reference rate; dated balance changes; compensation already paid; evidenced excess costs. For a narrower request, resolve only prerequisites that affect the requested branch, retaining established inputs; retrieve a historical statutory rate only for statutory interest, and check prior compensation or excess costs only when relevant. A current Bank Rate headline does not answer a historical debt. A principal payment does not erase accrued charges. A reduced balance does not create a new debt or compensation band.

## UK commercial charge rules

These deciding rules are inline so a reference read is not required for routine work. Read [the source register](references/uk-commercial-payments.md) for source detail, rate evidence, unusual timing or legal changes; recheck official guidance before a definitive current eligibility decision. If browsing is unavailable, use supplied official extracts or the dated snapshot and state the material verification gap without inventing current law.

- Confirm goods/services supplied in a UK commercial transaction and whether the debtor is a business or public authority. Consumer debts do not qualify under this scheme. Do not infer governing law from GBP or a UK address alone. Cross-border terms, old debts, acceptance/verification procedures and contested entitlement need clarification or appropriate advice before definite charges.
- Use the agreed valid due date. With no agreed payment date or acceptance procedure, the statutory relevant day is the last day of the 30-day period beginning with the later of invoice receipt/notice of the debt and delivery/service completion. Count that trigger date as day 1: relevant day = trigger + 29 days; interest starts the next day (trigger + 30 days). Do not add 30 days and then another day. The issue date is not proof of receipt. The 2015 EWNI and Scottish amendments supply this wording; it takes precedence over GOV.UK’s simplified “30 days after” summary. Public authorities usually have 30 days; business agreements usually 60, with a longer expressly agreed period possible if not grossly unfair. Do not automatically rewrite a supplied longer term. For an acceptance procedure, obtain the clause and completion date instead of blindly applying the ordinary default.
- First late day is the day after the due date. State the as-of/payment date. For a simple uninterrupted period, days = end date minus due date, with zero before/on the due date. Stop principal interest at payment; a paid-late invoice can still support a claim for its late period. Do not chase paid principal.
- Read the interest clause before claiming statutory interest. GOV.UK says a different contractual rate excludes the statutory rate; do not stack them. A disputed or possibly inadequate contractual remedy needs legal review, not a finding that a clause automatically removes all statutory rights. Public-authority terms cannot use a lower rate. Contractual calculations must follow the actual clause, including its day basis; do not assume simple annual interest if it is unstated.
- Statutory annual rate = 8 percentage points + the applicable Bank of England reference rate. The verified 2002 rate orders provide this start-date method for England/Wales/Northern Ireland and separately for Scotland. Select the reference date from when statutory interest first starts: starts in January–June → preceding 31 December; starts in July–December → preceding 30 June. Retrieve the dated rate history, not merely today's headline rate. Keep that annual rate for the debt; do not reset it merely at 1 January/1 July. Split balances at dated part-payments or credits. Use the unpaid VAT-inclusive original principal in each segment, with no compounding. Segment interest = principal × ((8 + base-rate-percent)/100) × days / 365. Keep precision until the final penny rounding. Show rates, dates, segment days, formula and total. For part-payments clarify allocation and when the balance changes; never guess disputed allocation.
- Statutory fixed recovery compensation is once for each qualifying late payment, never for each reminder: debt below £1,000 → £40; £1,000 to below £10,000 → £70; £10,000 or more → £100. Use the qualifying debt amount, not principal plus interest; do not switch bands merely because a later part-payment reduces the balance. Confirm the original debt and whether compensation was already claimed/paid. Do not assume one fresh charge per spreadsheet row, instalment or reissued charge invoice.
- Do not automatically add statutory compensation to a contractual-interest calculation. Fixed compensation arises when statutory interest begins on a qualifying debt (2002 regulations inserting section 5A); confirm that entitlement and the contract's recovery provisions. Document reasonable recovery costs; where the applicable legislation permits them, additional recovery = max(0, reasonable evidenced costs minus the fixed sum). Do not add both the fixed sum and the full same costs. The verified excess-cost amendments apply to contracts made from 16 March 2013 in England/Wales/Northern Ireland and 29 March 2013 in Scotland; verify old-contract applicability separately.
- For a genuinely disputed invoice, draft a request to resolve the disputed facts and check entitlement before asserting charges. OSBC guidance excludes invoices paid late due to a dispute; do not conclude that simply labelling a debt disputed extinguishes legal rights. Do not give blanket limitation-period advice across UK jurisdictions.
- If the user requests charge-invoice wording, draft a separate charge invoice/notice with its issue date, original invoice reference/date, principal used, late period, annual rate, interest and recovery charges separately itemised. Do not pretend it was issued or re-add the original principal as a second new debt. No interest or compensation on an interest-and-compensation invoice.
- Proposed Commercial Payments Bill reforms are not existing rights merely because announced. The verified Parliamentary record has Lords report on 15 September 2026 and third reading scheduled 20 October. Recheck passage, Royal Assent, commencement and applicable contract dates before changing the above rules.

## Worked example

Fictional input: Fennel Works Ltd supplied a UK business under a modern England-law contract, no different interest rate, no dispute or prior compensation. £2,400 including VAT was due 30 September 2026 and remains unpaid on 11 October. The official rate history checked for the reference date 30 June 2026 shows 3.75%.

Example output:

The supplied facts support statutory late-payment interest and one £70 recovery charge.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Unpaid principal | £2,400.00 |
| Interest through 11 October | £8.50 |
| Fixed recovery compensation | £70.00 |
| Total | £2,478.50 |

Days = 11 October − 30 September = 11. Annual rate = 8% + 3.75% = 11.75%, using Bank Rate on 30 June for July–December. Interest = £2,400 × 0.1175 × 11 / 365 = £8.49863, rounded to £8.50. Total = £2,400 + £8.50 + £70 = £2,478.50. [Bank of England rate history](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/boeapps/database/Bank-Rate.asp).

A £2,400 qualifying debt falls in the £70 recovery band; charge it once for that payment (GOV.UK). A separate charge invoice referencing the original invoice is needed if you choose to claim the charges.
