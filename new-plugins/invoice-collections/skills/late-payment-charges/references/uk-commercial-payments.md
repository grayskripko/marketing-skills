# UK commercial late payments: source register

Read 2026-10-11. Scope: UK goods/services commercial payments, not consumer lending, court interest or foreign-law debts. These are paraphrases, not copied manuals. Undated pages have no asserted publication date. Reopen official sources for current eligibility, rate-date evidence and changes before a current charge decision.

## Required official guidance

1. **When a payment becomes late**, GOV.UK. Loaded; no publication/update date displayed. https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery
   Deciding facts: commercial goods/services; usual agreed periods 30 days public authority / 60 days business; longer business terms require fairness. No agreement: the guide summarises 30 days from the later receipt/supply trigger. For exact statutory counting use item 14, not an extra day after trigger + 30. An invoice issue date alone does not establish receipt.
2. **Interest on late commercial payments**, GOV.UK. Loaded; no publication/update date displayed. https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery/charging-interest-commercial-debt
   Deciding facts: statutory annual interest adds eight percentage points to Bank Rate. Different contractual interest is a separate branch; public-authority contracts cannot use a lower rate. The worked government method uses annual interest divided by 365. Adding interest requires a new invoice. This page's headline link to current Bank Rate is supplemented by OSBC's reference-date guidance below.
3. **Claim debt recovery costs**, GOV.UK. Loaded; no publication/update date displayed. https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery/claim-debt-recovery-costs
   Deciding facts: one fixed sum per qualifying late payment, not per reminder. Debt <£1,000: £40; £1,000–<£10,000: £70; ≥£10,000: £100. Additional reasonable costs need the statutory excess-cost rule below, rather than duplicate reimbursement.

## Official calculation evidence

4. **Interest Calculator Guidance**, Office of the Small Business Commissioner. Loaded; no update date displayed. Original link redirects to current URL: https://www.smallbusinesscommissioner.gov.uk/help-and-guidance/interest-calculator/interest-calculator-guidance/
   Deciding facts: terms can be agreed verbally or in writing; receipt matters. Use VAT-inclusive original amount. Paid-late invoices can still incur charges. No interest or compensation on a charge invoice. Its dispute FAQ excludes invoices paid late due to a dispute; a contested case needs clarification rather than an automatic demand or a broad legal conclusion. It describes a six-year maximum; do not generalise this to jurisdiction-specific limitation advice. New charge invoices reference the original invoice and have their own issue date.
5. **OSBC: Interest Calculator**, GOV.UK algorithmic transparency record. Loaded. https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/osbc-interest-calculator
   Calculator guidance uses half-year reference dates and sums period calculations. For the legally deciding selection of a debt's rate, use the first-start rule in the official rate order below, rather than infer a reset for an existing debt from calculator implementation. This is rate evidence and calculation guidance, not an account integration or a guarantee that entering a form proves entitlement.
6. **Bank Rate history**, Bank of England. Loaded. https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/boeapps/database/Bank-Rate.asp
   Observed latest change: 18 December 2025, 3.75%; previous changes 7 August 2025, 4.00%, and 8 May 2025, 4.25%. No later change appears in the loaded table. Consequently 31 December 2025 and 30 June 2026 are 3.75% on this snapshot, yielding 11.75% statutory annual rate for both 2026 half-years. This is dated observed history, not a perpetual constant. Retrieve the latest change at or before the reference date determined by when interest first starts, then keep that rate for the debt. When a rate cannot be verified, show the formula and unresolved rate, not a fabricated total.

## Legislation and reform status

7. **Late Payment of Commercial Debts Regulations 2013**, official legislation PDF, made 21 February 2013, effective 16 March 2013. Loaded. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/395/pdfs/uksi_20130395_en.pdf
   Regulation 3 inserts section 5A(2A): additional reasonable recovery costs are only the excess over the fixed sum. Regulation 2 covers acceptance/verification procedures and longer-term fairness; do not reduce these cases to an invoice-date calculator. Instrument scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland, excluding contracts before commencement; it is not the Scottish instrument. HTML endpoints for section 5A and regulation 2 returned reader errors, so they are not recorded as verified. The Scottish counterpart is verified below. Obtain current consolidated provisions for unusual or contested timing; the 2013 timing text has later amendments, so it is not a standalone current-law timing calculator.

## Calculation conventions and boundaries

The skill uses simple daily interest with full precision until final nearest-penny rounding; this avoids the rounding in GOV.UK's simplified daily example. As-of date includes accrued late days through that date: a debt due 30 September is one day late on 1 October. Split balance changes without double-counting a boundary date, retain the debt's starting annual rate, and stop principal interest at payment. Allocation of part-payments and disputed amounts must come from evidence. These are transparent working conventions; unusual clauses or disputed legal day counts need case-specific verification.

Use this register to support the requested answer, not as an output template. Cite only sources that decide the case. Attribute supplied rate extracts to the user and dated legal material to this register; do not claim a fresh read unless it occurred. Where verification is incomplete, attach the material condition to the affected decision. Reminder tone, a concise deliverable, asking for a payment update and retaining missing-detail markers are editorial choices, not statutory demands. A proposed response date can make a firm dispute follow-up actionable without asserting an agreed or legal deadline. No automated sending, court-letter deadline, generic penalty percentage or right to seize assets is implied.

10. **Rate of Interest (No. 3) Order 2002**, legislation.gov.uk, effective 7 August 2002. Loaded; read 2026-10-11. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/1675/pdfs/uksi_20021675_en.pdf
    Article 4 selects the 30 June or 31 December immediately before statutory interest starts, according to the starting half-year. Keep that rate for the debt, rather than change it at later half-year boundaries. The original 1998 Act section 4(2) confirms interest uses the rate prevailing at the relevant day: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/20/pdfs/ukpga_19980020_en.pdf (loaded; read 2026-10-11). The original Act is not evidence of every subsequent amendment. Primary legislation takes precedence over a calculator's implementation when their wording differs.
11. **Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Scotland) Regulations 2013**, legislation.gov.uk, made 27 February 2013, effective 29 March 2013. Loaded; read 2026-10-11. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2013/77/pdfs/ssi_20130077_en.pdf
    Regulation 3 inserts the same excess-over-fixed-sum recovery rule for Scotland. Applies to contracts from commencement, not older contracts. Separate territorial instrument; do not extend the England/Wales/Northern Ireland instrument to Scotland.

12. **Rate of Interest (Scotland) Order 2002**, legislation.gov.uk, effective 7 August 2002. Loaded; read 2026-10-11. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2002/336/pdfs/ssi_20020336_en.pdf
    Scotland-only article 4 uses the same first-start reference-date selection as the England/Wales/Northern Ireland order. This establishes territorial support for the inline UK rate rule; it does not replace the need to check current amendments in a contested case.

13. **Late Payment of Commercial Debts Regulations 2002**, legislation.gov.uk, made 22 June 2002, effective 7 August 2002. Loaded; read 2026-10-11. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/1674/pdfs/uksi_20021674_en.pdf
    Regulation 2 inserts section 5A: fixed compensation arises when statutory interest begins on a qualifying debt, with £40/£70/£100 bands. This establishes why compensation cannot automatically be stacked onto a contractual-only calculation. This instrument extends to England/Wales/Northern Ireland and does not affect contracts before commencement; it is not the territorial source for Scottish amendments.

14. **2015 timing amendments**, legislation.gov.uk, read 2026-10-11. England/Wales/Northern Ireland: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1336/regulation/2/made ; Scotland: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2015/226/pdfs/ssi_20150226_en.pdf
    Official indexed text was retrieved; direct pages/PDF requests subsequently returned challenges or reader errors. Regulation 2 inserts section 4(2A)(b) and (2H): no agreed payment day means the last day of the relevant 30-day period; that period begins with the later/latest statutory trigger. Section 4(2) starts interest the next day. For ordinary receipt/supply with no acceptance process, count the trigger as day 1: relevant day = trigger + 29 calendar days; first interest day = trigger + 30. Example: receipt 6 September 2026, supply earlier → relevant day 5 October, first interest day 6 October. Agreed dates remain a separate branch. The original Act’s section 4(2) and former (5), independently loaded, also establish the inclusive-period structure; the 2015 text replaces (3)–(5), so use the amendments for modern subsection numbering.
