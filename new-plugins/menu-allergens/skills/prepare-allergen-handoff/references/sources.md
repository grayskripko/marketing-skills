## Deciding rules and sources

### FSA business guidance

URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/allergen-guidance-for-food-businesses/allergen-guidance-for-food-businesses
Read 2026-10-11. Sections 1, 2.4–2.6, 3.1–3.2, 4.1–4.2.

Non-prepacked dishes require ingredient allergen information. Oral provision requires visible written directions to obtain it. Written information plus conversation is recommended. Buffets need item-level information; phone/online sales need information before purchase completes and on delivery. Recipe changes need current ingredient records. Shared cooking oil can transfer allergens. Vegan does not establish freedom from animal allergens. In-person PPDS requires a food-name and full ingredients label with allergens emphasised; the distance-only exception below must be checked.

### FSA technical guidance

URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/food-allergen-labelling-and-information-requirements-technical-guidance/food-allergen-labelling-and-information-requirements-technical-guidance
Landing: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/food-allergen-labelling-and-information-requirements-technical-guidance
Read 2026-10-11. Served changelog includes March 2025 changes; scope England, Wales, Northern Ireland. Review text still says December 2024. Paragraphs 19–25, 31, 95–104 and footnote 5. Reopened 2026-10-11 during fixes.

Sulphite declaration threshold: greater than 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/L total SO2 in the ready-to-consume product. Gluten-free: no more than 20 mg/kg; very-low gluten: no more than 100 mg/kg. Oats remain declarable even if gluten-free. Precautionary labelling reflects a remaining cross-contact risk after risk assessment and controls; it does not substitute for controls.

Annex II exceptions are specific derivatives, not whole allergen categories: wheat glucose syrup/dextrose, wheat maltodextrins, barley glucose syrup and cereals for alcoholic distillates; fish gelatin carrying vitamin/carotenoid preparations and fish gelatin/isinglass used for beer/wine fining; fully refined soya oil/fat, listed soya-derived tocopherols, phytosterols/esters and stanol ester; whey for alcoholic distillates and lactitol; listed nuts for alcoholic distillates. Apply only when supplied product evidence matches the exact exception; uncertainty remains unresolved. Generic vegetable oil or soya oil does not prove refinement.

### Food Standards Scotland

URL: https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/business-guidance/running-a-food-business/packaging-and-labelling/allergen-labelling
Read 2026-10-11. Loaded; no update date displayed. Scotland. Sections “Providing food allergy information”, “Non-prepacked foods”, “Prepacked foods for direct sale”.

Scotland requires information about the 14 regulated allergens, with non-prepacked information available before purchase. Information may be written or given by staff. PPDS needs full ingredient labelling with allergenic ingredients emphasised. Do not describe the FSA England/Wales/Northern Ireland best-practice publication as Scotland-specific guidance.

## Field decisions

Keep recipe ID, supplier product, supplied date/version, exact label text, component and dish/variant together. “Contains” comes from ingredient evidence; “supplier warning” preserves the supplied precautionary statement; “preparation concern” comes from supplied kitchen facts; “unknown” identifies missing or contradictory evidence. Absence in a partial export never proves absence in the dish. These are authored workflow rules designed to prevent the evidence categories from being merged.

No source establishes the actual preparation controls, current supplier composition or approval for a user's dish. Obtain that evidence from the user's records. Do not silently apply a later label to an earlier batch.

### Food Standards Scotland training — field rules

URL: https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/business-guidance/running-a-food-business/tools-and-training/online-allergen-training-courses/online-allergen-training/online-allergen-training-module-1
Read 2026-10-11. Loaded; no update date displayed. Sections “Allergen rules”, “Know the law”, “Prepacked for direct sale”.

The regulated list includes specified cereals and nuts, with sulphites above 10 mg/kg or L in the finished product. Oral loose-food information requires signposting. PPDS classification depends on packing before offering for sale by the same business at the same premises/site or its mobile/temporary outlet; the food must be enclosed so its contents cannot be altered without opening or changing packaging. Full ingredient labelling is required. The training retains older EU/retained-law terminology, so use it for these field rules rather than as proof of current legislative terminology.

## Channel decision, reverified 2026-10-11

FSA technical guidance paragraphs 95–100: same business, premises/site (including its mobile/temporary outlet), enclosure and packing before offering for sale determine PPDS; another business packing and supplying the food is other prepacked food. Paragraphs 101–102: in-person PPDS has food name and full ingredient list with allergens emphasised. Paragraphs 103–104: distance-only PPDS is exempt from that national ingredients-list requirement, but allergen information is free before purchase and available at delivery. Apply obligations by route in mixed-channel operations. Footnote 5 excludes in-store electronic screens and drive-through screens from distance communication. These FSA conclusions cover England, Wales and Northern Ireland; consult the Scottish sources for Scottish cases.
