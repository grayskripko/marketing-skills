# Sources for mill certificate rules

Every page below was opened on 2026-10-12; entries marked 2026-10-13 were re-checked or replaced that day. The package makes no network requests at run time; these notes were embedded when it was built. Where a standard is sold (EN 10204, EN 10168, ASTM, ISO), only the official listing was opened and the full text was not; rules attributed to such a standard are taken from the listed official or inspection-body source that states them. Customer requirements, the purchase order, the governing code and the user's licensed specification decide acceptance.

## 1. EN 10204:2004 — identity, structure, document types

https://www.dinmedia.de/de/norm/din-en-10204/59682782 — read 2026-10-12. DIN EN 10204:2005-01 (German version of EN 10204:2004), status current, replaces DIN EN 10204:1995-08. Defines the types of inspection documents for metallic products; can also apply to non-metallic products; used with the product specification that sets technical delivery conditions.

https://shop.standards.ie/en-ie/standards/en-10204-2004-325038_saig_cen_cen_749388/ — read 2026-10-12. EN 10204:2004, CEN, published 2004-10-20, current. Clause list: 3 inspection documents based on non-specific inspection; 4 inspection documents based on specific inspection; 5 validation and transmission; 6 transmission of inspection documents by an intermediary; Annex A summary of inspection documents. Scope note: the list of information that may appear in inspection documents can be found in appropriate documents, for steel prEN 10168 (the abstract predates publication; the current EN 10168:2004 listing below confirms that scope).

https://www.evs.ee/en/evs-en-10204-2004 — read 2026-10-12. Main changes from the 1991 edition: new definitions (manufacturer, intermediary, product specification) and a reduced number of inspection document types.

https://assets.lrqa.com/m/65d9b900bbe96532/original/EN-10204-Type-3-2-Overview-Guide-GB-2023.pdf — read 2026-10-12 (LRQA, inspection body, 2023).
- EN 10204:2004 describes a type 3.2 certificate as prepared by both the manufacturer's authorised inspection representative, independent of the manufacturing department, and either the purchaser's authorised representative or the inspector designated by official regulations.
- Manufacturer = a party that carries out operations affecting the material properties of the finished product (examples: forge, heat treat); a stockist that only cuts, drills or grinds did not manufacture the material.
- "Intent of" 3.2: an inspection body's certificate for material that has only a 3.1, after witnessing additional tests; it must not be presented as a true 3.2, and its acceptability must be confirmed with the purchaser, customer or end user before work starts.

https://assets.lrqa.com/m/e73a9813e16addb/original/EN_10204_2004_Type_3-2_Certification_Guide_MY_2023.pdf — read 2026-10-12 (LRQA Malaysia guide; the document says © 2021, the 2023 in the file name is not confirmed as a revision year). In EN 10204:1991 the certificate requiring independent third-party involvement was called a type 3.1 certificate (3.1.A/3.1.C); it became type 3.2 in 2004. A 3.1 certificate under EN 10204:2004 is not validated by an independent third-party inspector.

https://assets.lrqa.com/m/5047d53014958e35/original/LRQA-EN1024-Navigating-the-landscape-US-2023.pdf — read 2026-10-12. Type 3.2 involves independent inspection and witnessing; ensures the product meets the order including traceability; "intent of" 3.2 is offered where material has only a 3.1, typically when bought from a stockist.

The catalogue headings above confirm only the two inspection categories (non-specific versus specific inspection). The detailed definitions of 2.1 (declaration of compliance, no results) and 2.2 (test report, results of non-specific inspection) are not shown in any listing that was opened; the full EN 10204 text was not opened, and a reviewer with a licensed copy should confirm the Annex A wording.

https://shop.standards.ie/en-ie/standards/en-10168-2004-324994_saig_cen_cen_749300 and https://codehub.building.govt.nz/assets/_generated_pdfs/bs-en-101682004-8385.pdf — read 2026-10-12. EN 10168:2004 lists information that may appear in steel inspection documents, with standard designations and code numbers for each section. The individual codes were not read and are not used by the skills.

## 2. Pressure Equipment Directive 2014/68/EU

https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014L0068 — read 2026-10-12. Consolidated version of 30 May 2026 (incorporating Directive (EU) 2024/2749), https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:02014L0068-20260530 — read 2026-10-13; Annex I 4.3 and 7.5 unchanged.
- Annex I 4.3: the equipment manufacturer must ensure the material conforms with the required specification; documentation prepared by the material manufacturer affirming compliance with a specification is required for all materials; for the main pressure-bearing parts of category II, III and IV equipment it must be a certificate of specific product control; where the material manufacturer has a quality-assurance system certified by a competent body established in the Union with a specific assessment for materials, the manufacturer's certificates are presumed to certify conformity.
- Annex I 7.5: unless other values are required, a steel is sufficiently ductile if elongation after rupture is not less than 14% and bending rupture energy on an ISO V test piece is not less than 27 J at a temperature not greater than 20 °C but not higher than the lowest scheduled operating temperature.

https://www.gov.pl/attachment/c0eb46a8-e093-4807-8a5d-637412bc75d9 — read 2026-10-12. "Guidelines related to the Pressure Equipment Directive 2014/68/EU", Commission Working Group "Pressure", version 5.1 of 29/10/2018, as hosted by the Polish government. Not legally binding; represents the unanimous opinion of Member States unless stated otherwise. Version 5.1 is historical: a 2025 bulletin of the Polish Office of Technical Inspection (https://www.udt.gov.pl/images/kampania/biuletyn/pdf/INSPEKTOR_3-4_2025.pdf, read 2026-10-13) cites version 6.0, document date 07/01/2019, last update 19/11/2024. The newest text was not opened; the guidelines below were accepted 2015–2016.
- G-04 (Annex I 3.1.5): traceability means range from physical marking to procedural methods, proportionate to the risk of mixing material grades; with no such risk, administrative means can suffice.
- G-05 (Annex I 4.3): the material manufacturer's affirmation of compliance with the specification and the order must be stated on or appended to the certificate, whichever type is issued. Note 1: a higher-level inspection document is always acceptable. Note 2: material from stockists must be accompanied by the material manufacturer's inspection documents. Note 7: the affirmation of compliance was not part of 3.1.B or 3.1.C under EN 10204:1991 but is part of 3.1 under EN 10204:2004.
- G-10: welding consumable manufacturers provide a 2.2 test report.
- G-27: when relying on a manufacturer's 3.1 under the third paragraph of 4.3, the equipment manufacturer keeps the material manufacturer's quality system certificate (field and range of validity, EU competent body, accreditation), or other equally objective evidence, in its technical documentation.
- G-28 (Annex I 7.5): 27 J refers to a 10 × 10 mm specimen (EN ISO 148-1); 7.5 mm or 5 mm sub-size specimens are recommended with an adjusted value (see EN 13445-2, EN 13480-2) when full size is not possible.
- G-29: the certificate of a material manufacturer with a certified quality-assurance system is presumed to certify conformity only to the extent specified in it; additional tests by the equipment manufacturer are supplementary, cannot "improve" certified properties, and no new material certificate is issued for them; not applicable to 3.2 where the material manufacturer performs the specified additional tests.
- G-30: an entity that only performs the mechanical tests on material made to chemistry only, and cuts to size, is not a material manufacturer and cannot affirm compliance, even if it issues an EN 10204 certificate naming itself as manufacturer.

## 3. IACS Unified Requirements (classification societies)

https://iacs.org.uk/resolutions/unified-requirements/ur-w/ur-w11-rev9-cln — read 2026-10-12. Current version UR W11 Rev.9 (May 2017); Rev.10 (Sep 2025) comes into force on 1 Jan 2027.

https://iacs.s3.af-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/18140622/ur-w11-rev9-may-2017-cln.pdf — read 2026-10-12. UR W11 Rev.9, normal and higher strength hull structural steels.
- 2.3: when steel is not produced at the works where it is rolled, a certificate stating the process, the steelmaker, the cast number and the ladle analysis is supplied.
- Table notes and 4.3: carbon equivalent from the ladle analysis, Ceq = C + Mn/6 + (Cr+Mo+V)/5 + (Ni+Cu)/15, applicable to carbon-manganese steels as a general indication of weldability; for TMCP steels Pcm = C + Si/30 + (Mn+Cu+Cr)/20 + Ni/60 + Mo/15 + V/10 + 5B may be used at the Society's discretion.
- 6.4: average of three impact tests must comply; one individual value may be below the average requirement if not less than 70% of it.
- 17.1: certificates contain at least the purchaser's order number, identification of the cast and piece including the test specimen number where appropriate, steelworks, grade, ladle analysis, condition of supply when other than as rolled, and test results.

https://iacs.s3.af-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/18135552/ur-w2rev3.pdf — read 2026-10-12. UR W2 Rev.3 (Sep 2021), test specimens and mechanical testing procedures.
- W2.4.3: a failed tensile test may be repeated with two further tests from the same piece; both must pass.
- W2.5.1–2: yield stress (upper yield) versus 0.2% proof stress Rp0.2 when no defined yield; Rp1 may be added for austenitic and duplex stainless steel.
- W2.5.4: elongation normally A5 on proportional gauge length 5.65√S0; for ferritic low or medium strength, not cold-worked steel, a required value on another gauge length L0 may after agreement be calculated as A0 = 2 A5 (√S0 / L0)^0.40.
- W2.7.2: sub-size Charpy averages: 10 × 7.5 mm → 5E/6; 10 × 5 mm → 2E/3; one value may be below the average if not less than 70%.
- W2.7.4: Charpy retest, where specified: three more specimens from the same material; new average of six must comply; not more than two values below the required average, of which not more than one below 70%.

## 4. United States federal rules

## Rules of thumb of this plugin (no external source)

- Absent and not-required results are reported separately; a missing limit is never filled from memory.
- Rounding: when rounding to the limit's decimal places changes the verdict, show both values and ask which rounding rule the specification uses.
- Carbon equivalent with an unreported element is shown as a lower bound (element taken as 0).
- Near-match heat numbers (one character different) are never linked automatically.
- Inconsistencies such as identical results on different heats or a missing test laboratory are raised as questions, not as findings.
