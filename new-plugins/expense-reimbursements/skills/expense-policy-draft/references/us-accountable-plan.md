# US employee accountable-plan reimbursement

Scope: US federal employee reimbursement arrangements. Source checks: **read 2026-10-11**. Before relying on a date-sensitive rule or rate, confirm that its effective period covers the expense and that the source is current for the requested decision. Refresh when the expense year, allowance method, or relevant federal rule changes, using the refresh procedure below. Publication year is not a claim that every rate applies to later travel. The eCFR page for §1.274-5 displayed Title 26 as up to date through October 7, 2026; this is the displayed currency date, not a publication date.

## Decision matrix

| Check | Concrete deciding rule | Source locator |
|---|---|---|
| Business connection | Allowable ordinary/necessary employee business expense; no wage substitution. Vague 'miscellaneous business expenses' is insufficient. | Publication 15 §5; §1.62-2(d), (e)(3). |
| Arrangement design | Employer must require business connection, timely substantiation and excess return. Voluntary receipts do not convert a nonaccountable design. | §1.62-2(c)(3). |
| Advance size | A confirmed failure can affect all payments under the affected arrangement, not merely the final excess; nonaccountable withholding applies when paid under (h)(2)(ii). Must be reasonably calculated not to exceed anticipated expenditures; timing and a zero final balance do not substitute for this condition. No percentage tolerance is supplied. | §1.62-2(c)(3), (f)(1), (h)(2)(ii), checked 2026-10-11. |
| Advance date | Within 30 days of when the expense is paid/incurred is a timing safe harbor. | §1.62-2(g)(2)(i); Publication 15 §5. |
| Accounting date | Within 60 days after expense payment/incurrence is a timing safe harbor. | Same. |
| Excess return | Within 120 days after expense payment/incurrence is a timing safe harbor. | Same. |
| Statement alternative | At least quarterly statements request accounting and/or return within 120 days of the statement. No silent substitution for an absent statement. | §1.62-2(g)(2)(ii). |
| Outside safe harbor | Facts/circumstances determine reasonableness; employer may treat amounts outside specified periods as untimely. Intentional overpayment practices to avoid withholding defeat safe-harbor use. | §1.62-2(g)(1), (g)(3), (h)(2). |
| Actual-cost lodging | Documentary evidence required at any amount, unless a valid exception applies. | §1.274-5(c)(2)(iii); Publication 463 ch. 5, printed p. 35. |
| Other covered travel | Documentary evidence at $75 or more, not merely above $75. Transportation exception when evidence is not readily available; under $75 is not an exemption from keeping expense elements. | Same. |
| Hotel bill fields | Name/location, stay dates, separate lodging, meals and other charges; supplement with business purpose. | Same; Table 5-1. |
| Meal evidence fields | Restaurant/location, date/amount, number served, nonfood charges; supplement business purpose and relationship. | Same; Publication 463 ch. 5. |
| Card-only evidence | Payment evidence may establish cost but not all expense elements or business purpose. Evaluate actual supplied fields; do not infer unseen itemization. | §1.274-5(c)(2)(iii)(B); Publication 463 ch. 5. |
| Missing/lost receipt | Request duplicate evidence; exceptional circumstances and losses beyond control can have reconstruction rules. An employee declaration alone does not automatically meet adequate accounting. | §1.274-5(f)(4); §1.274-5T(c)(4)/(5). |
| Other employee costs | Provide sufficiently specific nature and employer business connection. Do not generalize travel receipt thresholds to every expense. | §1.62-2(e)(3). |
| Mileage rate | 2025: $0.70; 2026: $0.725. Use trip date. Optional federal rate is not a universal employer reimbursement obligation. | Publication 463 ch. 4; Notice 2026-10 §3; IRS historical table. |
| Mileage fields | Business miles, time/date, destination/use and business purpose. No guessed mileage from a vague description. | §1.274-5T(b)(6); Publication 463 Table 5-1. |
| Commuting and vehicle costs | Ordinary home-to-regular-work travel is personal; business parking/tolls can be separate from standard mileage. Gas/maintenance is covered by the standard method for the same use. | Publication 463 ch. 4. |
| Meal reimbursement | Generally 50% employer deduction limitation does not halve an accountable-plan employee reimbursement. | Publication 463 ch. 2, 'Exception 2—employee's reimbursed expenses'; §1.62-2(h)(1). |
| Late excess | Under an otherwise accountable plan, unsubstantiated/unreturned excess becomes a payroll matter after the reasonable period; supported amounts remain distinct. | Publication 15 §5; §1.62-2(c)(2)(ii), (h)(2)(i)(A). |
| Per diem eligibility | Qualifying travel away from tax home requires absence beyond an ordinary workday and necessary sleep/rest; tax home generally means regular business location. Related parties cannot use §§4.01 or 5 under §6.07; do not extend that exclusion to separate §4.02. | Publication 463 ch. 1; Revenue Procedure 2019-48 §6.07, read 2026-10-11. |
| Per diem amount | Applicable allowance methods can deem amount substantiated. Still account for time, place and purpose. No numerical rate without official table, travel locality/date and selected method. | Revenue Procedure 2019-48 §7.01; Publication 463 ch. 6. |
| Excess allowances | Unsupported days/miles require return. The daily/mileage rate must be reasonably calculated not to exceed actual or anticipated expenses for the supported-unit return exception. Excess over deemed amount for supported units can be wages, without a mandatory return of that excess in every permitted allowance arrangement. | §1.62-2(f)(2); Publication 15 §5; Revenue Procedure 2019-48 §7.02–7.04. |
| Duplicate per diem coverage | Additional payment for the same covered costs has nonaccountable treatment; do not blindly stack receipts and allowances. | Revenue Procedure 2019-48 §6.06. |

## Per diem and special cases

This package does not embed a nationwide locality table or calculate high-low allowances. Ask for the employer's chosen method and a current official rate excerpt when numbers matter. Expense timing and rate-table effective periods are distinct; do not reuse the 2025 publication's allowance examples for October 2026.

Revenue Procedure 2019-48 §6.04 permits partial-day proration methods under stated conditions; do not treat 75% first/last day as the only possible private-employer method. Section 6.07 excludes related payor/employee arrangements from §4.01 and all §5 high-low methods, not the separate §4.02 M&IE-only method and uses a 10% ownership threshold for its §267(b)(2) reference. Where that issue matters, leave the allowance calculation for specialist review rather than assume eligibility. These limits do not prevent drafting supported actual-cost policy clauses.

## Working conventions, not federal mandates

Answer the requested question first in its requested shape. Row IDs, compact finding tables, explicit pending-evidence buckets and employee aliases are optional workflow choices, not required output sections. Use supplied complete records and business context; request only genuinely missing evidence. Employee policy drafts need concise substantiation and return obligations, not every receipt field or an invented finance procedure. The company selects approvals, caps, payment schedules and stricter deadlines. No default company cap, mandatory retention period or universal state-law conclusion is supplied.

Never classify an unpaid requested reimbursement as an already paid wage. Never certify an entire plan from a few sample receipts. Separate policy design, individual evidence, timing, and supported-versus-excess amounts.

## Refresh procedure

Check Publication 15's year, Publication 463's current edition, regulation changes and the expense-year mileage notice. For allowances, obtain the current official table and applicable method before computing. If a source fails, use a user-pasted official excerpt only for the scope and effective period it establishes; leave the disputed figure unresolved. Never replace an inaccessible official rule with an invented value. No private company or employee facts go into a network query.
