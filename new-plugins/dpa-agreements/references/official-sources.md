## GDPR — binding minimum

URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/
Adopted 2016-04-27; EUR-Lex consolidated text including corrigenda loaded. Read 2026-10-11. Relevant locators: Articles 4(7)/(8), 28(1)–(4), 28(9)/(10), 32, 33(1)/(2), 35/36 and Chapter V.

Article 28's contract fields cover activity, duration, purpose/nature, data and people categories and controller rights. Duties cover instructions, confidentiality, security, downstream authorisation/flow-down, assistance, controller-selected exit and audit information/inspections. The storage exception is limited to Union or Member State law. Compelled processing under such law requires advance notice unless that law prohibits notice on important grounds of public interest (28(3)(a)/(g)); other foreign obligations need separate analysis. Processor notice is without undue delay after awareness; 72 hours concerns controller-to-authority notice. Written form includes electronic form. No universal subprocessor notice interval, encryption algorithm or backup deletion period is specified here. Security measures are risk-dependent; examples include encryption, resilience/restoration and evaluating effectiveness.

## Controller–processor standard clauses — optional Article 28 instrument

URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2021/915/oj/eng
Decision dated 2021-06-04; current official decision text loaded, in force. Read 2026-10-11. Relevant locators: recital 10; Annex Clauses 2, 4, 6, 7.1–7.8, 8–10; Annexes I–IV.

Clause 2 preserves the standard text, allowing annex completion/updates and non-conflicting extra terms; Clause 4 gives clause precedence. Clause 7.6 covers audits using relevant certifications without extinguishing inspection rights. Clause 7.7 has specific/general authorisation options, a negotiated notice interval and a requested downstream-contract copy with permitted redactions. Clause 7.7(e) adds the downstream third-party-beneficiary exit right upon initial-processor disappearance, cessation or insolvency; 7.7(d) adds notice of downstream failure. Clause 8 covers forwarded rights requests and inaccurate-data notice; 9 covers breach assistance and notice contents. Annex IV is for specific authorisation. Annex I identifies parties; II describes processing; III requires specific security/assistance measures. Clause 10 covers suspension, termination and continued duties until exit; deletion certification is in 10(d). These are not Chapter V safeguards; a bespoke agreement can satisfy Article 28 without using them.

## Commission SCC Q&A — interpretive support

Requested PDF: https://commission.europa.eu/system/files/2022-05/questions_answers_on_sccs_en.pdf
May 2022 publication, 24 pages. Read 2026-10-11; loads and parses.
Publisher overview: https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/new-standard-contractual-clauses-questions-and-answers-overview_en
Read 2026-10-11; loads with controller–processor and transfer sections. Official overview still presents the two distinct clause sets and links the Q&A. Use the overview as the update checkpoint rather than interpreting the PDF directory date as proof of freshness.

Locators: Q&A section I on signature/modification and section II on controller–processor duties (pages 6–10); overview sections “Standard contractual clauses for controllers and processors in the EU/EEA” and international transfers. The first set addresses Article 28; the second addresses transfers under Chapter V. Standard texts can be incorporated in wider agreements with consistent additional protections, but cannot be altered so as to undermine the prescribed text. A contract titled “SCC” still needs its actual instrument, parties, options and annexes identified. Do not substitute a full transfer assessment for the supplier commitment review.

## EDPB 07/2020 — functional roles and concrete terms

Publisher page: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-072020-on-the-concepts-of-controller-and-processor-in-the-gdpr_en
Requested PDF: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf
Both read 2026-10-11; page marks final version and links the PDF. PDF is 51 pages, version 2.1; adopted 2021-07-07, minor corrections 2022-09-20. October 2023 is its file-path date, not adoption. No replacement of this final role guidance was found in the publisher check/search.

Locators: executive summary; Part I sections 2.1.4, 2.1.5, 2.2 and 3; Part II section 1, especially paragraphs 112 onward and 151–160 on downstream authorisation. Roles follow actual decisions. Essential means concern people, data, recipients and duration; practical means may be left to the processor. Own-purpose activity requires its own role analysis. Joint control requires joint determination, not cooperation alone. Contract terms should specify how duties are fulfilled rather than merely repeat GDPR. Specific authorisation requires a positive written decision; silence is not permission. Do not infer anonymisation from a de-identification label.

## Additional official source: EDPB Opinion 22/2024

Publisher: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/opinion-of-the-board-art-64/opinion-222024-on-certain-obligations-following-from-the_en
PDF: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_opinion_202422_relianceonprocessors-sub-processors_en.pdf
Read 2026-10-11; publisher page and PDF loaded; 34 pages. Adopted 2024-10-07; publisher page dated 2024-10-09. Relevant locators: executive summary; section 2.1.1, paragraphs 21–32; section 2.1.2; conclusions.

Controller visibility covers the whole processing chain, with names, addresses, contacts and activities readily available and current. Sufficient-guarantee verification applies regardless of risk, but its depth varies with risk. Initial processors should proactively maintain identity information. Controllers do not have a general duty to obtain every downstream contract; necessity for demonstrating compliance decides whether to request/review copies. The opinion does not erase a copy right in a chosen clause instrument. For downstream transfers, relevant exporter documentation should support the controller's assessment.
